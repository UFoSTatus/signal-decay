# Demonic and Inhuman Entities Across World Religions and Occult Traditions

## Christian Demonology: Fallen Angels and the Infernal Hierarchy

Christian demonology holds that demons are fallen angels — celestial beings who rebelled against God under Lucifer's leadership and were cast out of Heaven. They were never human and are considered inherently hostile to humanity.

The foundational grimoire for Christian demonology is the **Ars Goetia**, the first section of the *Lesser Key of Solomon* (Lemegeton), a 17th-century text cataloguing 72 demons with their ranks, seals, powers, and conjurations. [The 72 Demons of the Lesser Key Decoded](https://thalira.com/blogs/quantum-codex/goetia-meaning){evidence: "The Goetia is the first section of the Lemegeton (Lesser Key of Solomon), a seventeenth-century grimoire cataloguing 72 demons with their ranks, seals, powers, and conjurations."}

The Ars Goetia organizes these 72 demons into a strict feudal hierarchy mirroring a demonic court, with each rank corresponding to a classical astrological body:

- **Kings** (associated with the Sun) — The most powerful rulers of Hell. Bael is the first King of Hell. [List of demons in the Ars Goetia - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_demons_in_the_Ars_Goetia){evidence: "According to Wierus, Bael is the first King of Hell with estates in the east."}
- **Marquises** (associated with the Moon) — Including Samigina, Aamon, Leraje, Naberius, Forneus, Marchosias, Phenex, and others.
- **Presidents** (associated with Mercury) — Demons who command legions and impart knowledge.
- **Dukes** (associated with Venus) — Powerful nobles of Hell.
- **Earls/Counts** (associated with Mars) — Warrior-class demons.
- **Princes** (associated with Jupiter) — Rulers of specific infernal domains.
- **Knights** (associated with Saturn) — The sole knight, the lowest noble rank.

[List of demons in the Ars Goetia - Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/List_of_demons_in_the_Ars_Goetia){evidence: "Each ranking represents association with one of the local astrological bodies; Kings with the Sun, Marquises with the Moon, Presidents with Mercury, Dukes with Venus, Earls with Mars, Princes with Jupiter, and the sole Knight with Saturn."}

The system forms what one scholar describes as "a complete symbolic system: a hidden court of kings, dukes, princes, marquises, presidents, earls, and knights, each with a name, seal, appearance, function, and specialised field of influence." [The 72 Demons of the Goetia – OCCULT WORLD](https://occult-world.com/the-72-demons-of-the-goetia/){evidence: "The fascination with the seventy-two demons does not come only from fear. It comes from the fact that they form a complete symbolic system: a hidden court of kings, dukes, princes, marquises, presidents, earls, and knights, each with a name, seal, appearance, function, and specialised field of influence."}

The broader *Lesser Key of Solomon* describes how to protect oneself during conjuration. [Goetia (magic) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goetia_(magic)){evidence: "The Ars Goetia contains the names, orders, offices and seals of 72 demons, and educates on how to protect oneself when conjuring them."}

### The Seven Princes of Hell

An additional classification system assigns seven chief demons to the seven deadly sins. The *Lanterne of Light* (1409–1410) provided an early version, later refined by Peter Binsfeld in 1589:

1. **Lucifer** — Pride
2. **Mammon** — Greed
3. **Asmodeus** — Lust
4. **Leviathan** — Envy
5. **Beelzebub** — Gluttony
6. **Satan** — Wrath
7. **Belphegor** — Sloth

[Classification of demons - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_demons){evidence: "As part of his 1589 Treatise on Confessions by Evildoers and Witches, German theologian Peter Binsfeld prepared a classification of demons known as the Princes of Hell. Like the Lanterne of Light, Binsfeld used the seven deadly sins as a basis, though the two schemes differ in various ways and methodic construction."}

## Islamic Djinn: A Spectrum from Benign to Malevolent

Unlike Christian demons, Islamic djinn (jinn) are not inherently evil. They are a separate creation made from smokeless fire, possessing free will, and capable of being Muslim, Christian, Jewish, or disbelievers. The Quran explicitly states that some jinn accepted Islam. The classification of djinn types includes:

### Types of Djinn

**Ifrit (عفريت)** — Among the most powerful and dangerous types of djinn. They are described as powerful demons in Islamic culture, strong and cunning. [Ifrit - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifrit){evidence: "Ifrit, also spelled as efreet, afrit, and afreet (Arabic: عفريت, romanized: ʿifrīt), is a powerful type of demon in Islamic culture."}

**Marid (مَارِد)** — A type of rebellious, powerful djinn. The Arabic word means "rebellious" and they are considered chthonic demons similar in power to the Ifrit. [Marid - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marid){evidence: "A marid (Arabic: مَارِد, romanized: mārid) is a type of devil (shayṭān) in Islamic tradition. The Arabic word, meaning 'rebellious,' is applied to such supernatural beings. As a substantive it refers to a chthonic demon not much dissimilar to the ʿifrīt."}

**Shaitan (شَيْطَان)** — The most explicitly evil class. Shayatin are devils that incite humans and jinn to sin through whispering in their hearts. Their leader is Iblis (Satan in Islam), who refused to prostrate before Adam. [Shayatin - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayatin){evidence: "شَيْطَان shayṭān) refers to a class of evil spirits in Islam, inciting humans and jinn to sin by whispering (وَسْوَسَة waswasa) in their hearts (قَلْب qalb)."}

**Ghul (غول)** — Shape-shifting djinn associated with graveyards and desolate places. They lure travelers to their doom through constant metamorphosis. [Islam, Arabs, and the Intelligent world of the Jinn - Johns Hopkins University Press](https://muse.jhu.edu/chapter/392129/pdf){evidence: "It is mostly in the darkness of the night and in the desert, far from civilization, in the bottomless human fear, that ghouls manifest themselves, luring and seducing their victims by constant metamorphosis until they bring madness or death upon them."}

Islamic tradition also recognizes that djinn come in diverse physical forms. [Islam Question & Answer](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/2340){evidence: "Allah has created different types of jinn. Among them are some who can take on different forms, such as dogs and snakes; some who are like flying winds with wings; and some who can travel and rest."}

The overall classification system places these entities by power and moral nature. [Understanding the Types of Jinn](https://scientifically.blog/types-of-jinn-islamic-lore-guide){evidence: "Islamic tradition identifies several types of Jinn, primarily classifying them by their power and nature, including the powerful Ifrit, the rebellious Marid, the shape-shifting Ghul, and the devilish Shaitan."}

## Hindu Asuras and Rakshasas

Hindu cosmology contains two major categories of demonic or adversarial beings:

### Asuras

Asuras are powerful superhuman beings who oppose the Devas (gods). Unlike purely evil demons, they exist on a moral spectrum — some asuras are virtuous, though the majority are depicted as antagonistic. They are "part of Hinduism along with Yaksha (nature spirits), Rakshasa (fierce beings or demons), Bhuta (ghosts) and many more." [Asura - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asura){evidence: "Asura are part of Hinduism along with Yaksha (nature spirits), Rakshasa (fierce beings or demons), Bhuta (ghosts) and many more. Asura have been featured in many cosmological theories and legends in Hinduism and Buddhism."}

Asuras are frequently at war with the gods and are described as possessing immense power. [Asura - Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/asura){evidence: "Asuras are a class of beings opposed to the devas, or gods, in Hinduism. Asuras are often equally as powerful as the gods but are generally evil (although good asuras do exist), and in Hindu myths the gods frequently battle these demonic beings."}

They are also described as "powerful superhuman demigods or demons with good or bad qualities." [Hindupedia](https://www.hindupedia.com/en/Asura){evidence: "Asuras are described in Indian texts as powerful superhuman demigods or demons with good or bad qualities."}

### Rakshasas

Rakshasas are a distinct class of fierce, flesh-eating demons prominent in the Hindu epics (particularly the Ramayana, where the demon-king Ravana is a Rakshasa). They possess formidable shapeshifting abilities. [Rakshasa - Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/rakshasa){evidence: "rakshasa, in Hindu mythology, a type of demon that figures prominently in Hindu epic and Puranic literature. Rakshasas have the power to change their shape at will and appear as animals, as monsters, or, in the case of the female demons, as beautiful women."}

They are also called man-eaters and cannibals in Sanskrit tradition. [Rakshasa - New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Rakshasa){evidence: "A Rakshasa (Sanskrit: राँक्षसः, rā́kṣasaḥ) is a demon or unrighteous spirit in Hindu mythology. Rakshasas are also called man-eaters ('Nri-chakshas,' 'Kravyads') or cannibals. A female rakshasa is called a rakshasi, and a female rakshasa in human form is a manushya-rakshasi."}

Importantly, the term "rakshasa" does not map cleanly onto the Western concept of "demon." [Rakshasa Hindu Mythical Demon - Mythology Vault](https://mythologyvault.com/mythic-beings/spirits/rakshasa-hindu-mythical-demon/){evidence: "Rakshasa is a Sanskrit term for a class of powerful beings in Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain literature. English translations often use demon, but the category includes many forms and moral roles and does not map neatly onto the Christian devil."}

## Jewish Shedim

In Jewish demonology, **shedim** (שדים) are the primary class of demons. Unlike the Christian conception of demons as purely fallen angels, Jewish tradition presents a more complex picture where demons occupy a "middle world" between flesh and spirit.

The Talmud contains rich demonological traditions describing demons as ubiquitous but normally invisible beings. [Demons, Dybbuks, Ghosts, And Golems - Patheos](https://www.patheos.com/resources/additional-resources/2010/03/demons-dybbuks-ghosts-and-golems){evidence: "The Talmud has a rich, though vague, demonology. Houses of study are described as being filled with demons when sexual energy is not properly channeled. Great rabbis are able to perceive demons sitting on the right and left hands of every person."}

Jewish magical tradition held that the spiritual world was filled with vast numbers of these beings. [Jewish Magic and Superstition - Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms05.htm){evidence: "THE basis of Jewish magic lay the belief in a vast, teeming 'middle world,' a world neither of the flesh nor altogether and exclusively of the spirit. Demons and angels, to be counted only in myriads, populated that world; through their intermediacy the powers of magic were brought into operation."}

The shedim are identified as the prevailing class of demons in later Jewish demonology, analogous to the Greek *daimonia*. [Jewish Magic and Superstition - Sacred Texts](https://scrape.sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms24.htm){evidence: "In the later Jewish demonology the shedim are the hobgoblins, the prevailing class of demons. They are the δαιμόνια of the Greeks."}

A related class is the **mazzikin** (מַזִּיקִים) — invisible demons causing harm in daily life. [Mazzikin - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazzikin){evidence: "mazzikin, borrowed as Hebrew: מַזִּיקִים) are invisible demons that can create minor annoyances or greater dangers. This Aramaic term, which is found in the Talmud, means 'damagers' or 'those who harm'. It is generally understood to mean harmful invisible demons that a person could encounter in daily life."}

Notable Jewish demons include **Asmodeus** (Ashmedai), described as a king of demons in the legends of Solomon. [Asmodeus - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmodeus){evidence: "Asmodeus (/ˌæzməˈdiːəs/) or Ashmedai (/ˈæʃmɪˌdaɪ/; Hebrew: אַשְמְדָּאָי) is a king of demons in the legends of Solomon and the constructing of Solomon's Temple."}

## Buddhist Mara: The Personification of Spiritual Obstruction

In Buddhism, **Mara** is not a single entity but represents any malicious force that hinders enlightenment. Unlike Western demons who are inherently evil beings, Mara functions more as a cosmic principle of delusion and attachment.

Mara's influence extends over all beings trapped in Samsara (the cycle of rebirth), whether celestial or otherwise. [Mara (demon) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mara_(demon)){evidence: "Mara, in Buddhism, refers to any form of malicious force hindering enlightenment. Both demonic as well as celestial beings are part of Saṃsāra and hence, considered to be under Māra's influence, if they do not follow the message of the Buddha."}

Buddhist tradition identifies several aspects of Mara:
- **Devaputra Mara** — Mara as a celestial being who directly tempted the Buddha
- **Klesha Mara** — Mara as the embodiment of afflictive emotions
- **Skandha Mara** — Mara as the aggregates of existence that bind beings
- **Mrityu Mara** — Mara as death itself

## Paracelsus's Elemental Spirits

The Swiss physician and alchemist **Paracelsus** (1493–1541) established the most influential classification of elemental spirits in his work *Liber de Nymphis, Sylphis, Pygmaeis et Salamandris* (finished in the late 1530s, published posthumously in 1566). He assigned a class of beings to each of the four classical elements:

- **Gnomes** (also called Pygmaei) — Earth elementals, dwelling within the earth itself
- **Undines** (also called Nymphae) — Water elementals, inhabiting rivers, lakes, and seas
- **Sylphs** (also called Sylvestres) — Air elementals, invisible beings of the atmosphere
- **Salamanders** (also called Vulcani) — Fire elementals, dwelling in flames

[Elemental - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemental){evidence: "According to Paracelsus and his subsequent followers, there are four categories of elementals, which are gnomes, undines, sylphs, and salamanders. These correspond to the four Empedoclean elements of antiquity: earth, water, air, and fire, respectively."}

Paracelsus described these as liminal creatures sharing our world but not fully spirit nor fully flesh. [Undine - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undine){evidence: "Paracelsus believed that each of the four classical elements—earth, water, air and fire—is inhabited by different categories of elemental spirits, liminal creatures that share our world: gnomes, undines, sylphs and salamanders respectively."}

His naming system appears to include several of his own coinages, and the tradition is distinctly Renaissance rather than medieval. [The Four Spirits of Earth, Water, Air, and Fire](https://godsandmonsters.info/elemental/){evidence: "Gnomes, undines, sylphs and salamanders were set out as a system by Paracelsus in the sixteenth century, in the Liber de Nymphis, and several of the words appear to be his own coinages. The tradition is genuine, influential and Renaissance. It is not a survival from the Middle Ages."}

Importantly, elementals are **not demons** — they are morally neutral nature spirits without souls (according to Paracelsus), neither good nor evil by nature. They differ fundamentally from the hostile fallen angels of Christian demonology.

## Incubi and Succubi: The Sexual Parasites

**Incubi** (male) and **succubi** (female) are demons that approach humans during sleep for sexual purposes. They are classified as energy-draining entities that gradually weaken their victims.

The incubus "drains energy from the woman on whom it lies in order to sustain itself." [Incubus - New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Incubus){evidence: "In Western medieval legend, an incubus (plural incubi) is a demon in male form supposed to lie upon sleepers, especially women, in order to have sexual intercourse with them. They are also believed to do this in order to spawn other incubi. The incubus drains energy from the woman on whom it lies in order to sustain itself, and some sources indicate that it may be identified by its unnaturally cold penis. Religious tradition holds that repeated intercourse with such a spirit by either males or females (the female version of the incubus is called a succubus) may result in the deterioration of health, or even death."}

The concept originates from the idea of supernatural commerce with mortals, rooted in pre-Christian traditions. [Incubus/Succubus - Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/incubussuccubus){evidence: "A demon spirit that has sexual intercourse with mortals. The concept may have arisen from the idea of the commerce of gods with people, which was rife in pagan times. The male demon said to have intercourse with women is called the incubus and the female demon who seduces men the succubus."}

Medieval demonologist Alphonso de Spina (1467) included incubi and succubi as one of his primary categories of demons, and some traditions hold they could produce offspring (cambions). [Classification of demons - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_demons){evidence: "The belief in incubi and succubi (and their ability to procreate) seem to have inspired the fifth category, but it could also have been inspired in the Talmudic legend of demons having sexual intercourse with mortal women and men."}

## Psellus's Classification: Six Types of Demons by Domain

**Michael Psellus** (1018–1078 CE), the Byzantine scholar, prepared the influential *De operatione dæmonum* (On the Operation of Demons) in the 11th century. His taxonomy divided demons into six categories based on their elemental domain and habitat:

1. **Leliurium (Igneous/Empyreal)** — Fire demons dwelling in the upper atmosphere near the ethereal realm
2. **Aërial** — Demons of the air, inhabiting the atmosphere around humans
3. **Marine (Aqueous)** — Water demons dwelling in seas, rivers, and lakes
4. **Terrestrial (Earthly)** — Demons inhabiting the surface of the earth, forests, and fields
5. **Subterranean** — Demons dwelling beneath the earth in caves and underground places
6. **Lucifugous (Heliophobic/Light-Fleeing)** — The lowest and most malevolent class, demons that flee from all light and dwell in utter darkness

[Classification of demons - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_demons){evidence: "Michael Psellus prepared the influential De operatione dæmonum (On the Operation of Demons) in the 11th century, with a taxonomy dividing demons into six types: Leliurium (Igneous), Aërial, Marine (Aqueous), Terrestrial (Earthly), Subterranean, and Lucifugous (Heliophobic)."}

Psellus further distinguished between higher and lower demons in terms of their methods. [Psellus, Michael – OCCULT WORLD](https://occult-world.com/psellus-michael/){evidence: "Higher Demons act on the intellect, imagination, and senses, and lower Demons are animalistic, causing disease and bad luck and engaging in Possession. Lower Demons can speak, and they utter false prophecies."}

This system was adopted and adapted by later demonologists, appearing in the 17th-century *Compendium Maleficarum*. [The Engine of Oracles](https://engineoforacles.wordpress.com/2017/07/28/the-fire-demon/){evidence: "The early 17th century 'Compendium Maleficarum' identifies six different types of Demons – Fiery (or Empyreal), Aerial, Terrestrial, Water, Subterranean, and Lucifugous (light-fleeing)."}

## Demon vs. Malevolent Spirit: A Critical Distinction

A fundamental distinction exists in demonological and occult traditions between **demons** and **malevolent spirits**:

### Demons (Inhuman Entities)
- **Never were human** — they are a separate order of creation entirely
- In Christian theology: fallen angels who rebelled with Lucifer before humanity existed
- In Islamic theology: djinn created from smokeless fire as a distinct species
- Possess an inherent nature that is hostile, predatory, or adversarial toward humanity
- Operate with intelligence, hierarchy, and purpose
- Cannot be "saved" or redeemed in most theological frameworks
- Include the 72 Goetic spirits, Mara, asuras, and shayatin

### Malevolent Spirits (Once-Human Entities)
- **Were once living human beings** who died and became corrupted or trapped
- Includes ghosts, revenants, poltergeists, and the Jewish concept of the dybbuk (a displaced spirit possessing the living)
- Their malevolence often stems from traumatic death, unfinished business, or moral corruption during life
- May retain fragments of human personality, memory, or emotion
- Can sometimes be appeased, exorcised to rest, or redeemed through prayer
- Include ancestral spirits gone wrong, shades, and wraiths

The Talmudic tradition acknowledges this distinction by differentiating between *shedim* (demonic entities) and *mazzikin* (harmful spirits that may have different origins), while also recognizing ghosts (*ruchot*) as a separate phenomenon entirely.

King James I of England classified spiritual threats into four categories based on their method of interaction rather than their origin — acknowledging that "Lemures or Spectra" (spirits troubling houses) were distinct from possession demons. [Classification of demons - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_demons){evidence: "His classification was not based on separate demonic entities with their names, ranks, or titles, but rather categorized them based on four methods used by any given devil to cause mischief or torment on a living individual or a corpse."}

## Summary: Key Takeaways

1. **Demons are cross-cultural** — Every major world religion has a concept of hostile non-human spiritual entities, though their nature, origin, and moral status vary enormously between traditions.

2. **Not all are evil** — Islamic djinn have free will and can be righteous; Hindu asuras include virtuous beings; Buddhist Mara is more a cosmic principle than a personal devil. The binary "good vs. evil" framework is primarily a Christian demonological construct.

3. **Hierarchy matters** — The Goetic system organizes 72 demons into a feudal court (kings, dukes, princes, marquises, presidents, earls, knights), each rank tied to a planetary body, suggesting an ordered cosmos even in Hell.

4. **Elemental spirits are distinct from demons** — Paracelsus's gnomes, undines, sylphs, and salamanders are morally neutral nature beings, not fallen angels or hostile entities. They belong to a different ontological category.

5. **Psellus's six domains** — The medieval classification by habitat (Igneous, Aerial, Marine, Terrestrial, Subterranean, Lucifugous) influenced centuries of demonological thought and was adopted into later grimoires.

6. **The demon/spirit distinction is crucial** — A true demon was never human and possesses an alien intelligence; a malevolent spirit was once a living person and retains human characteristics. This distinction affects how practitioners approach exorcism, banishment, and protection.

7. **Energy drainage is a common theme** — From incubi/succubi draining life force to Buddhist Mara draining spiritual progress, the concept of parasitic spiritual entities transcends cultural boundaries.
