# Poltergeists — Deep Dive

## Word Origin: The "Noisy Ghost"

The word *poltergeist* is a direct borrowing from the German language, composed of two elements: **poltern** ("to make noise, to rumble, to rattle") and **Geist** ("ghost, spirit"). The compound translates literally as "noisy ghost," "rumble-ghost," or "loud spirit."

[Etymology, Origin & Meaning – Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/poltergeist){evidence: "'a noisy spirit, a ghost which makes its presence known by noises,' 1838, from German Poltergeist, literally 'noisy ghost,' from poltern 'make noise, rattle' (from PIE root *bhel- (4) 'to sound, ring, roar') + Geist 'ghost'"}

The term entered English around 1838–1850, though the concept it describes is far older. The earliest recorded use of the German compound appeared in 1540 in Erasmus Alberus's *Novum Dictionarii genus*.

[Poltergeist – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist){evidence: "The word poltergeist comes from the German language words poltern 'to make sound, to rumble' and Geist 'ghost, spirit' and the term itself translates as 'noisy ghost', 'rumble-ghost' or a 'loud spirit'."}

Encyclopædia Britannica defines it from the root components slightly differently, noting *Polter* as "noise" or "racket" rather than the verbal form:

[Britannica – Poltergeist](https://www.britannica.com/topic/poltergeist-occultism){evidence: "poltergeist, (from German Polter, 'noise' or 'racket'; Geist, 'spirit'), in occultism, a disembodied spirit or supernatural force credited with certain malicious or disturbing phenomena, such as inexplicable noises, sudden wild movements, or breakage of household items."}

The naming is significant: from the very beginning, the defining feature of the poltergeist was not visual apparition but *physical noise and disruption*—knockings, bangings, and the violent movement of objects.

## William Roll and RSPK (Recurrent Spontaneous Psychokinesis)

### The Man and His Theory

William G. Roll (1926–2012) was a parapsychologist who transformed the study of poltergeist phenomena from folklore into a testable hypothesis. Working primarily out of the Psychical Research Foundation and later at the Rhine Research Center, Roll spent decades investigating cases and developed the theoretical framework that would redefine the field.

[William G. Roll – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_G._Roll){evidence: "Roll is most notable for his belief in poltergeist activity. He coined the term 'recurrent spontaneous psychokinesis' (RSPK) to explain poltergeist cases."}

Roll coined the term RSPK specifically because the word "poltergeist" carried baggage—it implied a ghost, which was exactly the explanation he sought to move away from:

[Parapsychology Press](https://www.parapsychologypress.org/_files/ugd/ad0619_34dae6d2e50b4e0ead0330f8295bd6c4.pdf){evidence: "Because 'poltergeist' means 'noisy ghost,' which may be inaccurate, Bill instead used the term Recurrent Spontaneous Psychokinesis (RSPK) instead, and he used the term 'psi' to refer to all psychical activity."}

### The RSPK Framework

The RSPK hypothesis represents a fundamental departure from traditional supernatural explanations. Rather than attributing disturbances to ghosts, demons, or external spirits, Roll proposed that the physical disturbances originate from **a living person**—the "agent" or "focus person"—who unconsciously generates psychokinetic force.

[RSPK – Pararational](https://www.pararational.com/encyclopedia/rspk-recurrent-spontaneous-psychokinesis/){evidence: "The RSPK framework represents a significant departure from traditional interpretations of poltergeist activity. Rather than attributing the phenomena to ghosts, spirits, or external entities, the RSPK hypothesis proposes that the physical disturbances originate from a living person — typically called the 'agent' or 'focus person' — who unconsciously generates PK (Psychokinesis) that manifests as physical disturbances in the environment."}

The key observation that supported this hypothesis was that phenomena reliably ceased when the focus person was removed from the environment:

[RSPK – Pararational](https://www.pararational.com/encyclopedia/rspk-recurrent-spontaneous-psychokinesis/){evidence: "This hypothesis was developed through the observation that poltergeist cases frequently center on a specific individual, and the phenomena tend to cease when that person is removed from the environment."}

### Roll's Research Methodology

Roll personally investigated and documented numerous cases. His 1977 survey of 93 suspected RSPK agents found that 53% were under 20 years old. He also conducted laboratory PK tests with suspected agents, including Julio Vasquez from the Miami warehouse case (1967) and Tina Resch, both of whom showed statistically significant results:

[Poltergeist Phenomena: What Have We Learned?](https://docs.wixstatic.com/ugd/dd736b_8797179c9a4d49b4a035c8cadbf4fc15.pdf){evidence: "A survey by Roll (1977) found that of 93 suspected RSPK agents, 49 of them (53%)"}

Roll wrote extensively on the electromagnetic correlates of RSPK, proposing connections between brain activity and environmental disturbances:

[Poltergeists, Electromagnetism and Consciousness – Roll](http://lira.epac.to/DOCS-TECH/Physics/Consciousness%20Books%20Collection/W.%20Roll%20-%20Poltergeists,%20Electromagnetism%20and%20Consciousness.pdf){evidence: "I became interested in poltergeists or recurrent, spontaneous psychokinesis (RSPK) because it was the only convincing psi phenomenon I had witnessed at the time. I did not expect to learn anything profound about human nature or the cosmos."}

## The Adolescent Connection

### The Statistical Pattern

One of the most consistent findings across poltergeist research is the disproportionate involvement of adolescents, particularly girls undergoing puberty. This pattern was noted long before Roll's formal research—Victorian-era psychical researcher Frank Podmore proposed what he called the "naughty little girl" theory:

[Poltergeist – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist){evidence: "Psychical researcher Frank Podmore proposed the 'naughty little girl' theory for poltergeist cases (many of which have seemed to centre on an adolescent, usually a girl)."}

### Why Adolescents?

The RSPK model offers a compelling explanation: adolescents—especially those going through puberty—experience extreme hormonal shifts, intense emotional upheaval, feelings of powerlessness, and repressed rage or anxiety. The theory proposes that when normal emotional outlets are blocked, psychic energy externalizes as physical disruption.

[RSPK – Pararational](https://www.pararational.com/encyclopedia/rspk-recurrent-spontaneous-psychokinesis/){evidence: "The agent is most commonly an adolescent or young adult, frequently female, who is experiencing significant psychological stress, repressed anger, or emotional turmoil. Roll and other researchers proposed that the inability to express or process intense emotions through normal channels leads to an unconscious externalization of psychic energy that produces physical effects."}

Cases frequently correlate with family conflict, social marginalization, and situations where the adolescent feels trapped or unheard:

[Quora – Poltergeist Activity](https://www.quora.com/How-do-you-explain-poltergeist-activity-Is-it-a-building-up-of-psychic-energy-or-is-there-something-else-going-on){evidence: "Cases often center on an adolescent (especially pubescent) in emotional turmoil—stress, family conflict, or social marginalization correlate strongly with poltergeist reports. This pattern supports psychosocial explanations (attention seeking, dissociation, or unconscious motor manifestations)."}

### The Brovetto-Maxia Hypothesis

In 2008, Italian physicists Pierluigi Brovetto and Vera Maxia published a controversial paper proposing a physical mechanism by which pubescent brains might generate disturbances:

[New Scientist – 'They're here': The mechanism of poltergeist activity](https://www.newscientist.com/article/1908425-theyre-here-the-mechanism-of-poltergeist-activity/){evidence: "Brovetto and Maxia hypothesise that the changes in the brain that occur at puberty involve fluctuations in electron activity that, in rare cases, can create disturbances up to a few metres around the outside of the brain."}

Their paper, published on arXiv, proposed that transformations in pubescent brains could reduce molecular bond strength through vacuum polarization effects:

[arXiv – Brovetto & Maxia](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.0382v2){evidence: "A simple analysis of phenomenology of these disturbances shows that they might have a common origin, that is, a reduction in strength of molecular bonds due to an enhancement in polarization of vacuum which decreases the actual electron charge. Arguments based on Prigogine' nonequilibrium thermodynamics are proposed, which show how transformations in brain of some pubescent children or young women might be the cause of these effects."}

## Famous Cases

### The Enfield Poltergeist (1977–1979)

Perhaps the most well-documented poltergeist case in history occurred at 284 Green Street in Brimsdown, Enfield, North London. The activity centered on two adolescent sisters—Janet (aged 11) and Margaret Hodgson (aged 13)—living with their single mother Peggy and two younger siblings.

[Enfield Poltergeist – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enfield_poltergeist){evidence: "The Enfield poltergeist was a claim of supernatural activity at 284 Green Street, a council house in Brimsdown, Enfield, London, England, between 1977 and 1979. The alleged poltergeist activity was centred on sisters Janet, aged 11, and Margaret Hodgson, aged 13."}

The case was investigated by Maurice Grosse and Guy Lyon Playfair of the Society for Psychical Research. Phenomena included knockings, furniture movement, objects flying across rooms, and most remarkably, a gruff male voice emanating from 11-year-old Janet claiming to be the spirit of a deceased former resident:

[The Horror Collection – Enfield Poltergeist](https://thehorrorcollection.com/the-enfield-poltergeist-case/){evidence: "Centered on the Hodgson family—particularly 11-year-old Janet Hodgson—the disturbances included furniture moving on its own, loud knocking sounds, objects flying through the air, and a gruff voice claiming to be the spirit of a deceased former resident."}

The BBC reported that police were called to the home at the onset:

[BBC – The Enfield Poltergeist](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20231026-the-enfield-poltergeist-why-the-unexplained-mystery-that-shocked-1970s-britain-continues-to-disturb){evidence: "In August 1977, the police arrived at 284 Green Street in the north London suburb of Enfield. Peggy Hodgson, a single mother of four, reported that her two young daughters – Janet, aged 11, and older sister Margaret – had heard strange knocking. The source of the sound could not be determined."}

The Psi Encyclopedia records that over thirty independent witnesses observed phenomena:

[Enfield Poltergeist – Psi Encyclopedia](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/enfield-poltergeist/){evidence: "In 1977, a house in the north London suburb of Enfield was the scene of violent disturbances similar to what has been reported in poltergeist-type episodes – knockings and other noises with no apparent cause, doors opening and closing by themselves, small objects hurled across rooms, and suchlike."}

The Enfield case is significant because it appeared to develop *personality*—the voice claimed identity, answered questions, and displayed apparent intelligence—raising the question of whether RSPK can become sentient (discussed below).

### The Bell Witch (1817–1821)

The Bell Witch of Adams, Tennessee is one of the oldest and most dramatic poltergeist cases in American history. Activity centered on the Bell family, particularly daughter Betsy Bell (age 12 at onset), and included knockings, bedclothes being pulled off, physical assaults (slapping, hair-pulling, scratching), and eventually a disembodied voice that identified itself as "Kate" and displayed remarkable intelligence—carrying on conversations, quoting scripture, and expressing hatred toward family patriarch John Bell.

The entity reportedly grew in power over four years, eventually claiming responsibility for John Bell's death by poisoning in 1820. It is one of the earliest cases where poltergeist activity appeared to develop full sentience and personality, making it a critical case in the debate over whether poltergeists are unconscious human energy or independent entities.

### The Rosenheim Poltergeist (1967)

The Rosenheim case is considered one of the most rigorously documented poltergeist incidents because it occurred in a professional office environment and was investigated by physicists. In the fall of 1967, electrical and physical disturbances began in the law office of Sigmund Adam in Rosenheim, Bavaria:

[Rosenheim Poltergeist – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenheim_poltergeist_claim){evidence: "Bender alleged that electrical and physical disturbances in the office of the lawyer Sigmund Adam were caused by the telekinetic powers of 19-year-old secretary Annemarie Schaberl."}

Parapsychologist Hans Bender documented that paintings and overhead light fittings swung, fluorescent tubes unscrewed themselves, and massive spikes in electrical activity occurred—all in the presence of 19-year-old secretary Annemarie Schaberl:

[Manchester Metropolitan University](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/story/eight-things-you-need-know-about-poltergeists-just-time-halloween){evidence: "Strange things started to happen in the presence of the 19 year-old secretary Annemarie Schaberl. Paintings and overhead light fittings started swinging, while fluorescent tubes unscrewed themselves and massive spikes in electrical activity occurred."}

The case demonstrated the person-centered nature of poltergeist activity perfectly: Schaberl had recently suffered a serious personal relationship trauma and exhibited non-specific neuroses. When she was sent on vacation, the activity stopped. When she returned, it resumed. She was ultimately dismissed:

[Monstropedia – Rosenheim Poltergeist](https://monstropedia.org/index.php?title=Rosenheim_Poltergeist){evidence: "After questioning Ms Schneider, it was discovered that she had recently gone through a serious personal relationship trauma. It was also noted that Ms Schneider suffered from non-specific neuroses. Interestingly, once she was sent on vacation the poltergeist activity stopped. Annemarie Schneider was dismissed from the company when the events began anew after she returned. There are no records of any further poltergeist activity since then."}

## Poltergeist vs. Demon: A Critical Distinction

### The Core Difference

The fundamental distinction between a poltergeist and a demon lies in their **origin**:

- **Poltergeist** = energy generated by a **living human being** (the focus person), expressed unconsciously as physical disturbances.
- **Demon** = an **external, non-human entity** that has never been human, operating with intelligence and malicious intent.

[Ghost vs Poltergeist vs Demon – Strange & Twisted](https://strangeandtwisted.com/blogs/how-to-guides/how-to-identify-a-haunting-ghost-vs-poltergeist-vs-demon){evidence: "Poltergeist activity centers on a living person rather than a location, which means clearing the house does nothing and the focus of help needs to shift entirely. Demonic activity requires professional religious intervention and is genuinely worsened by amateur attempts at communication or confrontation."}

### Behavioral Differences

| Feature | Poltergeist | Demon |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Origin | Living human energy | External non-human entity |
| Follows people | Always (the focus person) | Yes (the target) |
| Location-bound | No—moves with the agent | No—attaches to individuals |
| Response to religion | No effect | Intensifies or flees |
| Escalation | Peaks then subsides | Consistent escalation |
| Resolution | Psychological support for agent | Professional religious intervention |
| Intelligence | Usually limited/impersonal | Highly strategic and deceptive |

[Higgypop – Demon vs Spirit](https://www.higgypop.com/news/demon-vs-spirit/){evidence: "Unlike normal hauntings, demonic hauntings aren't the results of deceased human spirits or negative human energy."}

### The Practical Implication

This distinction is not merely academic—it determines the response. Cleansing a house or performing an exorcism does nothing for poltergeist activity because the source is internal to the living person. Conversely, providing therapy to a demonically oppressed individual addresses a symptom, not the cause. Getting the classification wrong can make either situation dramatically worse.

## The Theory: Poltergeists Aren't Spirits At All

### Unconscious Telekinetic Outbursts from the Living

The RSPK hypothesis proposes that poltergeist phenomena are not caused by any external spirit, ghost, or entity whatsoever. Instead, they are **unconscious psychokinetic outbursts** from a living person under extreme psychological stress.

[Psi Encyclopedia – Psychological Aspects](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/psychological-aspects-poltergeist-cases/){evidence: "When poltergeist disturbances cluster around one person, researchers consider the possibility that they are responsible for the disturbances through unconscious psychokinesis."}

The Psi Encyclopedia's overview of poltergeist research frames it clearly—the "mischievous intent" often attributed to a spirit may simply be the emotional state of the agent projected outward:

[Poltergeists Overview – Psi Encyclopedia](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/poltergeists-overview){evidence: "The commotion often appears to have a mischievous intent, hence the traditional tendency to associate it with ghosts or other supernatural beings. However, in many cases it may equally be attributed to a force emanating from a living person, typically a child who exhibits symptoms of repressed emotion."}

The Rhine Research Center, which continues Roll's work, describes RSPK as the "modern interpretation" of what was previously called poltergeist activity:

[Rhine Research Center](https://www.rhineonline.org/psychokinesis){evidence: "RSPK is the modern interpretation of what was previously called poltergeist activity. It occurs when an individual – in this case an adolescent boy – is present in every case where a poltergeist-like effect is observed."}

### The Psychodynamic Model

Roll drew on psychodynamic psychology to explain the mechanism: intense emotions (rage, grief, frustration, fear) that cannot be expressed through normal behavioral channels become "dammed up" psychic energy. This energy discharges unconsciously through psychokinesis—the mind acting directly on matter without conscious intent or awareness.

The agent is almost always unaware they are the source. In many cases, they are as frightened as everyone else, genuinely believing an external force is at work. This unconsciousness is a key feature—it distinguishes RSPK from deliberate fraud.

## Physical Manifestations

Poltergeist phenomena are defined by their physicality. Unlike apparitions (which are primarily visual) or intelligent hauntings (which are primarily atmospheric), poltergeists *move matter and generate measurable physical effects*.

### Documented Manifestation Types

[Poltergeist – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist){evidence: "Most claims or fictional descriptions of poltergeists show them as being capable of pinching, biting, hitting, and tripping people. They are also depicted as capable of the movement or levitation of objects such as furniture and cutlery, or noises such as knocking on doors."}

The full range of reported physical manifestations includes:

**1. Object Movement and Throwing**
Objects moving, levitating, or being hurled across rooms with considerable force. Small objects (stones, ornaments, cutlery) are most common, but furniture displacement is documented in severe cases.

**2. Spontaneous Fires**
Small fires igniting without apparent physical cause—one of the most dangerous manifestations.

**3. Water Appearing**
Puddles, dripping, or streams of water appearing from no identifiable source, including in rooms with no plumbing.

**4. Rappings and Knockings**
The most common manifestation—persistent knocking, banging, or rapping sounds with no identifiable physical origin. These were historically the first phenomena reported and gave the poltergeist its name.

**5. Physical Assaults on People**
Scratches, bites, slaps, pinches, and bruising appearing on the bodies of occupants, particularly the focus person.

**6. Electrical Disturbances**
Lights flickering, appliances malfunctioning, phone systems disrupted (as in the Rosenheim case).

[The Horror Collection – What Is a Poltergeist?](https://thehorrorcollection.com/what-is-a-poltergeist-definition-manifestations-and-history/){evidence: "A poltergeist is used to describe a series of disruptive, usually short-lived physical disturbances that have no obvious physical cause — loud raps and knocks, small objects moving or flying through the air, furniture shifting, doors opening and closing, and occasionally more extreme events like small fires or water appearing from nowhere."}

[Poltergeists Overview – Psi Encyclopedia (SPR)](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/poltergeists-overview){evidence: "Noisy outbreaks of thrown objects, raps, fires and apparent mischief have been reported for centuries, yet their interpretation remains unsettled."}

The arXiv paper by Brovetto and Maxia catalogued the range scientifically:

[arXiv – Brovetto & Maxia (2008)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.0382v2){evidence: "Accounts about poltergeist are common to different ages and different countries around the world. They concern certain strange spontaneous phenomena, such as rappings, burning of fabrics, failure of electric equipments, movement of objects, for which there is no acceptable explanation."}

## Can Poltergeist Activity Become Sentient?

### The Central Question

If poltergeists are merely unconscious telekinetic discharges from a living person, how do we explain cases where the activity appears to develop intelligence, personality, and autonomous will? This is one of the most troubling questions in poltergeist research.

### Cases Where RSPK Appeared to Develop Personality

**The Bell Witch (1817–1821):** What began as simple knockings and bedclothes disturbances escalated into a fully voiced entity that named itself "Kate," held lengthy conversations, demonstrated knowledge of distant events, quoted scripture, and expressed complex motivations (hatred of John Bell, affection for his wife Lucy). If this was RSPK from 12-year-old Betsy Bell, her unconscious mind created something that appeared entirely autonomous.

**The Enfield Poltergeist (1977–1979):** Simple knockings and furniture movement escalated into a voice—deep, gruff, and male—speaking through 11-year-old Janet Hodgson. The voice identified itself as "Bill" (later determined to match a man who had died in the house), answered questions, made jokes, and cursed. It displayed apparent personality distinct from Janet's.

**The Rosenheim Case:** While this case did *not* develop apparent sentience—remaining impersonal electrical and physical disruption—its clean cessation upon Schaberl's departure supports the pure RSPK model. The contrast with Enfield and Bell Witch is instructive: not all RSPK develops personality.

### Theoretical Explanations

Several theories attempt to reconcile RSPK with apparent sentience:

1. **The Tulpa/Thoughtform Theory:** Intense, sustained psychokinetic output may create a semi-autonomous psychic construct—a "tulpa" in Tibetan Buddhist terminology—that develops its own rudimentary personality from the agent's unconscious material.

2. **Dissociative Identity Explanation:** The "personality" is actually a dissociated fragment of the agent's own psyche, expressing itself through PK the way dissociative identity disorder expresses through behavior. The voice isn't an external entity gaining sentience—it's a split-off aspect of the agent's own mind.

3. **The Escalation Hypothesis:** RSPK begins as simple energy discharge but, if unresolved, the agent's unconscious begins to *organize* the output—giving it direction, intent, and eventually a constructed identity as a coping mechanism.

4. **The External Entity Theory (Competing):** Skeptics of the pure RSPK model argue these cases prove that at least *some* poltergeist phenomena involve genuine external entities that are drawn to or empowered by the agent's psychic energy, using it as a "battery."

## The Lifecycle: Onset, Escalation, Peak, Cessation

### A Characteristic Pattern

Poltergeist cases follow a remarkably consistent lifecycle, distinguishing them from other types of paranormal phenomena:

[Unmasking the Noisy Ghost – Arab Psychology Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.arabpsychology.com/poltergeist/){evidence: "The events are typically short-lived, ranging from a few days to several months, before ceasing abruptly, distinguishing them from long-term, chronic haunting claims."}

### Phase 1: Onset

Activity begins subtly—unexplained noises, small objects found in wrong positions, vague feelings of unease. The trigger is typically a period of escalating emotional stress in the agent: family breakup, school bullying, onset of puberty, grief, or repressed trauma reaching critical mass.

### Phase 2: Escalation

Phenomena become more frequent, more violent, and more varied. What began as knockings progresses to object movement, then to thrown objects. New manifestation types appear (fires, water, scratches). Activity becomes undeniable to all household members. This phase often coincides with external attention (investigators, media), which may increase stress on the agent and feed the cycle.

### Phase 3: Peak

The most dramatic phenomena occur—furniture thrown across rooms, physical assaults, possible voice manifestation, apparent intelligence. This is typically the shortest phase. The agent's psychological state is at maximum crisis.

### Phase 4: Sudden Cessation

Perhaps the most distinctive feature of poltergeist cases: they stop. Often abruptly, without explanation. The Strange & Twisted entity classification chart captures this pattern:

[Ghost vs Poltergeist vs Demon – Strange & Twisted](https://strangeandtwisted.com/blogs/how-to-guides/how-to-identify-a-haunting-ghost-vs-poltergeist-vs-demon){evidence: "Escalation pattern: [Poltergeist] Peaks and subsides"}

Cessation correlates with one or more of the following:
- The agent is removed from the environment
- The underlying emotional crisis resolves (family situation changes, puberty stabilizes, therapy begins)
- The agent reaches adulthood and develops better emotional regulation
- In some cases, the activity simply exhausts itself as the agent's psychic "charge" depletes

This self-limiting nature is one of the strongest arguments for the RSPK model over the external entity model—if poltergeists were spirits, there would be no reason for them to simply stop.

## Summary

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Etymology matters:** The name "poltergeist" (noisy ghost) reflects centuries of assumption that the phenomena are caused by spirits—an assumption that modern research challenges fundamentally.

2. **RSPK reframes the phenomenon:** William Roll's Recurrent Spontaneous Psychokinesis theory proposes that poltergeist activity originates not from the dead but from the living—specifically from unconscious psychokinetic discharges by a focus person under extreme emotional stress.

3. **The adolescent pattern is robust:** Across cultures and centuries, poltergeist cases disproportionately center on adolescents (especially girls) going through puberty, emotional turmoil, or family crisis. The hormonal and psychological upheaval of adolescence appears to be a key triggering condition.

4. **Physical manifestations are dramatic and varied:** Objects thrown, fires started, water appearing, knockings, electrical disturbances, and physical assaults (bites, scratches, slaps) represent the full spectrum of documented phenomena.

5. **Poltergeists are not demons:** The distinction is fundamental—poltergeists are theorized as human-generated energy (resolved through psychological support for the agent), while demons are external non-human entities (requiring religious intervention). Getting this classification wrong can worsen the situation.

6. **The sentience question remains unresolved:** Cases like Enfield and Bell Witch, where RSPK appeared to develop autonomous personality, challenge the pure unconscious-discharge model. Whether this represents dissociated fragments of the agent's psyche, emergent thoughtforms, or genuine external entities using the agent's energy as a power source remains one of the most debated questions in the field.

7. **The lifecycle is self-limiting:** Poltergeist activity follows a characteristic arc—onset, escalation, peak, sudden cessation—typically lasting days to months rather than years. This self-limiting nature strongly supports the living-agent hypothesis over the spirit hypothesis.
