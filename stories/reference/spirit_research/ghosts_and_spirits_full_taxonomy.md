# Ghosts and Spirits — Full Taxonomy of Paranormal Manifestations

## Residual Hauntings

### The Stone Tape Theory

Residual hauntings are the most commonly reported type of paranormal phenomenon and function like a recorded loop of past events — an environmental playback with no consciousness behind it. The theoretical framework most often invoked to explain them is the **Stone Tape Theory**, a pseudoscientific hypothesis proposing that ghosts and hauntings occur when traumatic events are imprinted on certain stones or other materials and are subsequently replayed, similar to a magnetic tape recording [Stone Tape theory - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Tape_theory){evidence: "The Stone Tape theory is a pseudoscientific claim that ghosts and hauntings occur when traumatic events are imprinted on certain stones or other materials and are subsequently replayed, similar to a magnetic tape recording."}.

The premise holds that bedrock or building stone — particularly quartz-rich rocks, limestone, and even bricks — can act like natural recording devices, capturing emotional energy from traumatic events [Skeptical Inquirer](https://skepticalinquirer.org/newsletter/spooky-rocks/){evidence: "The premise of the stone tape concept is that bedrock or building stone of the location 'captured' emotional energy from a traumatic event."}. Under the right conditions, these recordings "play back" as apparitions, sounds, or feelings perceived by witnesses [The Stone Tape Theory - The Horror Collection](https://thehorrorcollection.com/what-is-the-stone-tape-theory/){evidence: "Materials such as stone, wood, or quartz might hold these imprints. Later, under the right conditions, they play back as apparitions, sounds, or feelings."}.

The theory is often linked with the concept of **place-memory** — the idea that locations themselves retain recordings of past events. This concept was explored by Eleanor Sidgwick of the Society for Psychical Research, who postulated that buildings or locations might retain recordings of past events, particularly traumatic or remarkable ones, which could then be replayed [Hauntings, Residual Energy & Sound Memory](https://krill-nectarine-67h8.squarespace.com/journal/stone-tape-theory){evidence: "She postulated that place memory could explain certain types of supernatural activity. Sidgwick argued that buildings or locations might retain 'recordings' of past events, particularly traumatic or remarkable ones, which could then be 'replayed.'"}.

The name itself derives from a 1972 BBC television play called *The Stone Tape*. Since its broadcast, the hypothesis of residual haunting — that ghosts are recordings of past events made by the natural environment — has come to be widely known by this name [The Stone Tape - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Tape){evidence: "Since its broadcast, the hypothesis of residual haunting — that ghosts are recordings of past events made by the natural environment — has come to be known as the 'Stone Tape Theory'."}.

### Characteristics of Residual Hauntings

Residual hauntings are characterized by:

- **Repetition**: The same footsteps, the same figure on the stairs, the same door opening at the same hour
- **No awareness or interaction**: The phenomenon does not respond to living observers
- **Location-bound activity**: Events are tightly tied to a specific place
- **Historical correlation**: Activity often connects to documented trauma, routine, or significant events at the location

A residual haunting requires no intervention whatsoever; anything done to address it is at best pointless and at worst disruptive to a space that would have settled on its own [Strange and Twisted](https://strangeandtwisted.com/blogs/how-to-guides/how-to-identify-a-haunting-ghost-vs-poltergeist-vs-demon){evidence: "A residual haunting requires no intervention whatsoever: anything you do to address it is at best pointless and at worst disruptive to a space that would have settled on its own."}.

## Intelligent Hauntings

In contrast to residual phenomena, intelligent hauntings involve spirits that demonstrate awareness and responsiveness to living observers [Paraghosts - Residual vs. Intelligent Hauntings](https://www.paraghosts.com/residual-vs-intelligent-hauntings-how-to-tell-the-difference/){evidence: "The two primary classifications are residual hauntings, which function like a recorded loop of past events, and intelligent hauntings, where spirits demonstrate awareness and responsiveness."}.

### Characteristics of Intelligent Hauntings

Intelligent activity typically manifests as:

- **Timed responses to questions** (via EVP, spirit boxes, or physical manipulation)
- **Behavior that changes with different people present**
- **Selective interaction with objects** (trigger objects moving only when specific conditions are met)
- **Apparent awareness of being observed**

An intelligent haunting may respond well to direct communication and boundary setting, or it may escalate if approached incorrectly [Strange and Twisted](https://strangeandtwisted.com/blogs/how-to-guides/how-to-identify-a-haunting-ghost-vs-poltergeist-vs-demon){evidence: "An intelligent haunting may respond well to direct communication and boundary setting, or it may escalate if approached incorrectly."}.

These entities appear to retain personality, memory, and will. They can choose when and how to interact, and their activity is not bound to repetitive loops but to conscious decision-making.

## Apparitions

### Full-Body Apparitions

Full-body apparitions are among the rarest and most dramatic reported phenomena — the visual manifestation of a complete human figure, sometimes so solid-looking that witnesses initially mistake them for living people. Hauntings can involve a range of experiences, from subtle sensory cues like sudden cold spots or faint whispers to more dramatic events such as full-bodied apparitions appearing [The Horror Collection - Types of Hauntings](https://thehorrorcollection.com/what-are-the-types-of-hauntings/){evidence: "Factually, hauntings can involve a range of experiences, from subtle sensory cues like sudden cold spots or faint whispers to more dramatic events such as objects moving on their own or full-bodied apparitions appearing."}.

### Partial Apparitions

Far more common than full-body manifestations are partial apparitions — half-formed figures, disembodied hands, faces at windows, or torsos without limbs. These suggest incomplete manifestation, as though the energy required to produce a full visual form exceeds what is available.

### Crisis Apparitions

Crisis apparitions represent one of the most extensively documented categories in psychical research. These are cases in which a person reports a hallucinatory experience — visual or otherwise — which apparently represents someone at a distance, subsequently considered to have coincided with that person's death or a significant life event [Apparitional experience - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparitional_experience){evidence: "These are cases in which a person reports having a hallucinatory experience, visual or otherwise, which apparently represents someone at a distance; this experience subsequently being considered to have coincided with that person's death, or a significant life event of some kind."}.

When paranormal researchers began investigating such experiences at the end of the 19th century, they termed them "crisis apparitions" — when people have visions of (or sense the presence of) friends or relatives around the time of their death, even though they are physically distant [Psychology Today - Encounters at the Time of Death](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/out-of-the-darkness/202204/encounters-at-the-time-of-death){evidence: "When paranormal researchers began investigating such experiences at the end of the 19th century, they termed them 'crisis apparitions' — when people have visions of (or sense the presence of) friends or relatives around the time of their death, even though they are physically distant."}.

A key evidential feature is that in crisis apparition stories, the dying or just-dead person visits a neighbour, friend, or close relative, and the percipient declares what they have seen even before confirmation of the death has arrived [Communicating with the Dead Over Space and Time - Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14780038.2023.2258606){evidence: "In crisis apparition stories, the dying or just-dead person visited a neighbour, friend or close relative and a key feature was that the percipient would declare what they had seen, even before confirmation had arrived of the death."}.

If the temporal coincidence between the crisis and the distant apparitional experience cannot be explained by conventional means, parapsychologists presume some unknown form of communication — such as telepathy — has taken place [Encyclopedia.pub - Apparitional Experience](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/history/show/79620){evidence: "If the temporal coincidence of the crisis and the distant apparitional experience cannot be explained by any conventional means, then in parapsychology the presumption is made that some as yet unknown form of communication, such as telepathy, has taken place."}.

## Orbs

A ghost orb (also called a spirit orb) is a round, glowing, semi-transparent ball of light that appears in photographs, video footage, or — much more rarely — to the naked eye, usually in locations associated with hauntings or strong emotion, such as cemeteries, battlefields, old buildings, or funerals [The Horror Collection - What Is a Ghost Orb?](https://thehorrorcollection.com/what-is-a-ghost-orb/){evidence: "A ghost orb (also called a spirit orb) is a round, glowing, semi-transparent ball of light that appears in photographs, video footage, or — much more rarely — to the naked eye, usually in locations associated with hauntings or strong emotion, such as cemeteries, battlefields, old buildings, or funerals."}.

Orbs are typically white (though colours can vary), typically pale (though can be bright), typically spherical (though can be other shapes), and are most commonly caught on compact digital cameras [ASSAP - Testing Orb Zone Theory](https://assap.ac.uk/articles/detail/testing-orb-zone-theory){evidence: "Now known ubiquitously as orbs, these anomalies are typically white (though colours can vary), typically pale (though can be bright), typically spherical (though can be other shapes) and typically caught on compact digital camera."}.

However, the scientific consensus among paranormal investigators themselves is skeptical. The Society for Psychical Research notes that paranormal investigators consider the phenomenon to have normal causes, mainly the effect of light reflected by dust, pollen, and other airborne material [Psi Encyclopedia - Orbs (Light Anomalies)](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/orbs-light-anomalies/){evidence: "Paranormal investigators consider the phenomenon to have normal causes, mainly the effect of light reflected by dust, pollen and other airborne material."}.

## Ectoplasm

Ectoplasm is a concept from early spiritualism referring to a physical substance supposedly extruded from the body of a medium during séances, through which spirits could manifest visually or physically. The term was invented by Nobel laureate and French physiologist Charles Richet around 1894, marking a shift in how communications from the spirit world were understood [University of Colorado - Bawdy Technologies and the Birth of Ectoplasm](https://www.colorado.edu/gendersarchive1998-2013/2011/09/01/bawdy-technologies-and-birth-ectoplasm){evidence: "On or about 1894, communications from the spirit world changed with the emergence of ectoplasm, a term invented by Nobel laureate and French physiologist Charles Richet."}.

Ectoplasm was described as a white or grey viscous substance that could take the form of faces, hands, or full figures. It was central to physical mediumship in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, though the vast majority of ectoplasm cases have since been exposed as fraud — mediums using cheesecloth, paper, egg white, and other materials to simulate the phenomenon.

## Shadow People

### What They Are

A shadow person (also known as a shadow figure or black mass) is a being resembling an animate human shadow, sometimes interpreted as the presence of a spirit [Shadow person - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_person){evidence: "A shadow person (also known as a shadow figure or black mass) is a popular subject in circles of the paranormal and the supernatural, detailing beings resembling animate human shadows, sometimes interpreted as the presence of a spirit."}.

Shadow people are described as dark silhouettes with human shapes and profiles that flicker in and out of peripheral vision, and witnesses have reported the figures attempting to "jump on their chest and choke them" [Shadow person - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_person){evidence: "Hollis describes shadow people as dark silhouettes with human shapes and profiles that flicker in and out of peripheral vision, and claims that people have reported the figures attempting to 'jump on their chest and choke them'."}.

### Why They Appear as Shadows

Several physiological and psychological conditions account for the shadow-like appearance:

- **Sleep paralysis**: A sufferer may perceive a shadowy or indistinct shape approaching them when they lie awake paralyzed
- **Heightened emotion and fear**: A person walking alone in darkness may incorrectly perceive a patch of shadow as an attacker
- **Sleep deprivation**: Many methamphetamine addicts report shadow people after prolonged periods without sleep
- **Deliriant substances**: Shadow people are commonly reported under the effects of substances such as datura, diphenhydramine (Benadryl), and benzydamine

Science has linked this phenomenon to various sleep and psychiatric disorders, as well as the abuse of drugs like Benadryl, but paranormal enthusiasts and several world religions have another name for these entities: shadow people [All That's Interesting - Shadow People](https://allthatsinteresting.com/shadow-people){evidence: "Science has linked this phenomenon to various sleep and psychiatric disorders, as well as the abuse of drugs like Benadryl, but paranormal enthusiasts and several world religions have another name for entities like the Hat Man: shadow people."}.

### The Hat Man Phenomenon

The Hat Man is a specific and widely reported variant of shadow person — a dark, featureless, shadowy figure wearing a wide-brimmed hat (often a fedora, bowler, or top hat) and sometimes a long coat. Hat Man witnesses all report seeing the same thing: a shadow being whose features are completely obscured except for the presence of a large, wide-brimmed hat [All That's Interesting - The Hat Man](https://allthatsinteresting.com/the-hat-man){evidence: "Hat Man witnesses all report seeing the same thing: a shadow being whose features are completely obscured except for the presence of a large, wide-brimmed hat."}.

The phenomenon is strongly associated with sleep paralysis — a form of parasomnia where many people see visual hallucinations despite being awake, and a lot of people report the same experience of seeing a shadowy figure sporting a hat [IFLScience - The Hatman](https://www.iflscience.com/have-you-seen-the-hatman-reports-of-this-creepy-sleep-paralysis-figure-are-surprisingly-widespread-78630){evidence: "A form of parasomnia, this haunting sleep condition is a kind of waking nightmare as many people see visual hallucinations despite being awake. Weirdly, a lot of people report the same experience of seeing a shadowy figure, and even more strangely still, this 'demon' is often sporting a hat."}.

The Hat Man commonly appears when the person is psychically most vulnerable — half asleep, in the hypnagogic state between waking and sleeping [Pararational - The Hat Man](https://www.pararational.com/the-hat-man-unraveling-the-shadowy-figure-haunting-our-nightmares/){evidence: "Commonly seen at night, the Hat Man typically appears when you are psychically most vulnerable, half asleep."}.

Scientists attribute the phenomenon to sleep paralysis, when "the brain wakes up before the body does" and the person is unable to move, while believers maintain it is a genuine paranormal entity [The Week - Who is the Hat Man?](https://theweek.com/97457/who-is-the-hat-man-the-nightmare-shared-by-dreamers-across-the-world){evidence: "The phenomenon is known as a 'shadow person', a concept that believers say is a ghost, but scientists attribute to sleep paralysis, when 'the brain wakes up before the body does' and the person is unable to move."}.

The Hat Man is also commonly associated with the abuse of diphenhydramine (Benadryl). Descriptions date back to as early as the late 2000s [Shadow person - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_person){evidence: "The Hat Man is commonly associated with sleep paralysis and the abuse of the antihistamine medicine diphenhydramine, commonly sold under the brand name Benadryl."}.

## Crisis Ghosts

Crisis ghosts are a subset of crisis apparitions specifically focused on the moment of death. They appear to loved ones at the precise moment the person dies, often across great physical distances. This has been one of the most studied phenomena in parapsychology since the founding of the Society for Psychical Research in 1882.

Crisis impressions, deathbed visions, sensed presences, and terminal lucidity all cluster around moments when ordinary explanations seem, for many witnesses, unexpectedly strained or incomplete [Psi Encyclopedia - Phenomena Related to Danger, Death, and Bereavement](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/phenomena-relating-danger-death-bereavement/){evidence: "Crisis impressions, deathbed visions, sensed presences, terminal lucidity and near-death numinosity all cluster around moments when ordinary explanations seem, for many witnesses, unexpectedly strained or incomplete."}.

In a landmark study conducted between 1959 and 1973, parapsychologists Karlis Osis and Erlendur Haraldsson reported that 50% of the tens of thousands of individuals they studied in the United States and India had experienced deathbed visions [Deathbed phenomena - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathbed_phenomena){evidence: "In a study conducted between 1959 and 1973, parapsychologists Karlis Osis and Erlendur Haraldsson reported that 50% of the tens of thousands of individuals they studied in the United States and India had experienced deathbed visions."}.

The Society for Psychical Research's foundational work treated apparitions as methodologically diverse phenomena, ranging from crisis cases and purposeful encounters to hauntings, hallucinations, and experimentally induced experiences [Psi Encyclopedia - Ghosts and Apparitions in Psi Research](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/ghosts-and-apparitions-psi-research-overview/){evidence: "It treats apparitions as methodologically diverse phenomena, ranging from crisis cases and purposeful encounters to hauntings, hallucinations and experimentally induced experiences."}.

## Anniversary Ghosts

An anniversary ghost is a spirit or haunting that recurs on the same date each year, often tied to the anniversary of a traumatic or meaningful event like a death, murder, or tragedy [Ghostly Activities - Anniversary Ghosts Explained](https://ghostlyactivities.com/anniversary-ghosts-explained-a-guide-for-ghost-hunters/){evidence: "An anniversary ghost is a spirit or haunting that recurs on the same date each year, often tied to the anniversary of a traumatic or meaningful event like a death, murder, or tragedy."}.

Anniversary hauntings are considered a specialized form of residual haunting — the environmental "recording" activates only under specific temporal conditions. Famous examples include:

- **The Tower of London**: Anne Boleyn's ghost reportedly appears on the anniversary of her execution (May 19)
- **Battlefield hauntings**: Gettysburg and other Civil War sites report increased activity on battle anniversaries
- **The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall**: Connected to specific calendar dates

These phenomena blur the line between residual and intelligent hauntings because the precise timing suggests either an extraordinarily specific environmental trigger or some form of residual consciousness aware of temporal cycles.

## The Distinction: Ghost vs. Spirit

### Ghosts — Tied to Place, Trauma, and Residual Energy

In paranormal investigation, a ghost is often treated as the lingering presence, imprint, or earthbound consciousness of a deceased person [Paraghosts - Ghosts vs. Spirits](https://www.paraghosts.com/whats-the-difference-between-ghosts-and-spirits-in-paranormal-investigation/){evidence: "In paranormal investigation, a ghost is often treated as the lingering presence, imprint, or earthbound consciousness of a deceased person, while a spirit is usually viewed as a more aware, responsive, and nonphysical entity that can interact intentionally."}.

Ghosts are understood as:
- **Unconscious energetic impressions** left behind in space and time
- **Entities stuck between worlds**, unable to find resolution
- **Location-bound** presences tied to the place of death, trauma, or strong emotional attachment
- **Energy masses that have not left the physical realm** once their time in their physical body was complete

Ghosts are energy masses that have not left the physical realm once their time in their physical body was complete — a ghost can be any residual energy lingering in a space after whatever created it has gone [Amanda Linette Meder](https://amandalinettemeder.squarespace.com/blog/2014/1/15/ghosts-vs-spirits){evidence: "Ghosts are energy masses that have not left the physical realm once their time in their physical body was complete. A ghost can be any residual energy lingering in a space after whatever created it, has gone."}.

### Spirits — Consciousness That Has Moved On But Can Return

A spirit, by contrast, is usually a conscious nonphysical presence with some level of awareness, intent, and responsiveness. In simple terms, ghosts are often described as tied to a place or event, while spirits are often described as more intelligent and communicative [Paraghosts - Ghosts vs. Spirits](https://www.paraghosts.com/whats-the-difference-between-ghosts-and-spirits-in-paranormal-investigation/){evidence: "In simple terms, ghosts are often described as tied to a place or event, while spirits are often described as more intelligent and communicative."}.

Spirits are understood as:
- **Conscious beings capable of awareness, communication, and interaction**
- **Entities with agency** around their interaction with the living, whether as guides or companions
- **Free to roam** — not bound to one location
- **Able to return intentionally** to communicate with the living

In simple terms, ghosts are unconscious energetic impressions left behind in space and time, while spirits are conscious beings capable of awareness, communication, and interaction [Human Amplified - The Difference Between Spirits and Ghosts](https://humanamplified.com/blog/i-am-still-here-the-difference-between-spirits-and-ghosts-and-how-theyre-tied-to-our-humanity){evidence: "In simple terms, ghosts are unconscious energetic impressions left behind in space and time, while spirits are conscious beings capable of awareness, communication, and interaction."}.

The distinction between ghosts and spirits lies primarily in their intent and state of being — ghosts are entities stuck between worlds, unable to find resolution, while spirits have agency around their interaction with the living [Ceremonie - Ghost vs. Spirit](https://shopceremonie.com/blog1/what-is-the-difference-between-a-spirit-and-a-ghost){evidence: "The distinction between ghosts and spirits lies primarily in their intent and state of being. Ghosts are often understood as entities that are stuck between worlds, unable to find resolution, while spirits are believed to have agency around their interaction with the living, whether as guides or even spirit companions."}.

### Sensory Differences

The two are also encountered differently:
- **Ghosts** are frequently reported as visual/ambient anomalies — cold spots, shadows, visual manifestations
- **Spirits** are more often encountered through intuition, dreams, inner voice, ritual-mediated communication, or emotional impressions

Ghosts tend to stay connected to earth while spirits freely roam the spiritual realm [Ask Mooks - Difference Between Ghost and Spirit](https://askmooks.substack.com/p/difference-between-ghost-and-spirit){evidence: "Ghosts tend to stay connected to earth while spirits freely roam the spiritual realm."}.

## Cultural Variations of Ghosts and Spirits

### Japanese Yūrei (幽霊)

Yūrei are figures in Japanese folklore analogous to the Western concept of ghosts. The name consists of two kanji: 幽 (yū), meaning "faint" or "dim," and 霊 (rei), meaning "soul" or "spirit" [Yūrei - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%ABrei){evidence: "Yūrei (幽霊) are figures in Japanese folklore analogous to the Western concept of ghosts. The name consists of two kanji, 幽 (yū), meaning 'faint' or 'dim' and 霊 (rei), meaning 'soul' or 'spirit'."}.

In Japanese tradition, ghosts (obake or yūrei) appear in ancient folklore and literature, usually in moral tales designed to both warn and entertain, but they were also an important element of ancestor worship. If deceased family members were not honoured, they could bring havoc to the daily lives of those who had forgotten them [World History Encyclopedia - Ghosts in Ancient Japan](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1059/ghosts-in-ancient-japan/){evidence: "Ghosts (obake or yurei) appear in ancient Japanese folklore and literature, usually in moral tales designed to both warn and entertain but they were also an important element of ancestor worship. If the deceased members of a family were not honoured, they could bring havoc to the daily lives of those who had forgotten them."}.

Yūrei have only been considered frightening since the early modern era. In ancient and medieval Japan, when there was considerable "commerce" between the people of this world and spirits, the word yūrei typically referred to the souls of the dead, who neither showed themselves nor enacted curses [Nippon.com - Fear and Reverence](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g02176/){evidence: "Yūrei have only been considered frightening since the early modern era. In ancient and medieval Japan, when there was considerable 'commerce' between the people of this world and spirits, the word yūrei typically referred to the souls of the dead, who neither showed themselves nor enacted curses."}.

Japanese ghost culture also includes **goryō** — spirits of nobility who died as a result of political intrigue and who, because of their ill will for the living, brought about natural disasters, diseases, and wars [Britannica - Goryō](https://www.britannica.com/topic/goryo-Japanese-religion){evidence: "In the Heian period (ad 794–1185) goryō were generally considered to be spirits of nobility who had died as a result of political intrigue and who, because of their ill will for the living, brought about natural disasters, diseases, and wars."}.

Key yūrei categories include:
- **Onryō** (怨霊) — vengeful spirits who return to harm the living
- **Ubume** (産女) — spirits of women who died in childbirth
- **Funayūrei** (船幽霊) — ghosts of those who died at sea
- **Zashiki-warashi** (座敷童子) — childlike house spirits

### Chinese Hungry Ghosts (餓鬼)

A hungry ghost in Buddhism and Chinese traditional religion is a being driven by intense emotional needs in an animalistic way. The Chinese name 餓鬼 (èguǐ; "hungry ghost") translates the Sanskrit *preta* in Buddhism [Hungry ghost - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_ghost){evidence: "A hungry ghost in Buddhism and Chinese traditional religion is a being who is driven by intense emotional needs in an animalistic way. The Chinese name 餓鬼; èguǐ; 'hungry ghost' translates the Sanskrit preta in Buddhism."}.

Hungry ghosts play a role in Chinese Buddhism, Japanese Buddhism, Taoism, and Chinese folk religion. They are distinct from generic ghosts or damnation — 鬼 (guǐ), the residual spirit of a deceased ancestor [Wikiwand - Hungry Ghost](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Hungry_Ghost){evidence: "Hungry ghosts play a role in Chinese Buddhism, Japanese Buddhism, Taoism, and in Chinese folk religion. Hungry ghosts are distinct from generic ghosts or damnation, 鬼; guǐ (the residual spirit of a deceased ancestor)."}.

Found throughout the Far East, from the Philippines to Japan, China, Thailand, Laos, Burma, India, and Pakistan, they are universally described as human-like wraiths with mummified skin, narrow withered limbs, grossly bulging stomachs, long thin necks, and tiny mouths [Kashgar - Hungry Ghosts: Their History and Origin](https://kashgar.com.au/blogs/tribal-culture/hungry-ghosts-their-history-and-origin){evidence: "Found in every part of the Far East, from the Philippines to Japan and China, Thailand, Laos, Burma, India and Pakistan, they are universally described as human-like wraiths with mummified skin, narrow withered limbs, grossly bulging stomachs, long thin necks and tiny mouths."}.

In Asian culture, the seventh moon of the year (typically late August to late September) is when hungry ghosts are believed to enter the mortal realm — this period is celebrated as the **Hungry Ghost Festival** [YMAA - Remember to Feed the Hungry Ghosts](https://ymaa.com/articles/2025/08/remember-feed-hungry-ghosts){evidence: "In Asian culture, the seventh moon of the year is when hungry ghosts enter mortal realm. This period typically goes from late August to late September."}.

### Irish Banshee (Bean Sídhe)

The banshee ("woman of the fairy mound") is one of Ireland's most iconic supernatural figures — a female spirit whose wailing cry (the *keen* or *caoine*) foretells an imminent death in the family she is attached to. Unlike most ghosts, the banshee is not the spirit of a dead person but a supernatural being from the fairy realm who has attached herself to certain ancient Irish families.

Key characteristics:
- Appears as an old woman in grey or white, or sometimes as a beautiful young woman
- Her cry is heard before a death — not after
- She is tied to specific bloodlines, particularly old Gaelic families (the O'Neills, O'Briens, O'Connors, O'Gradys, and Kavanaghs)
- She represents a liminal being — between the fairy world and the human world

### Mexican La Llorona (The Weeping Woman)

La Llorona is a vengeful ghost in Hispanic American folklore who is said to roam near bodies of water mourning her children she drowned in the river [La Llorona - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Llorona){evidence: "La Llorona (Latin American Spanish: [la ʝoˈɾona]; 'the Crying Woman, the Weeping Woman, the Wailer') is a vengeful ghost in Hispanic American folklore who is said to roam near bodies of water mourning her children she drowned in the river."}.

According to legend, she was a woman named Maria who, driven by jealousy, rage, or despair over her husband's infidelity, drowned their two sons in a river and then herself [Britannica - La Llorona](https://www.britannica.com/topic/La-Llorona){evidence: "According to legend, she was a woman named Maria who, driven by jealousy, rage, or despair over her husband's infidelity, drowned their two sons in a river and then herself."}.

The legend has deep pre-Columbian roots. The presence of phantasmal beings that weep near rivers is a recurring characteristic of Mesoamerican mythology. Historical accounts from 16th-century colonial Mexico record: "The people heard a weeping woman night after night. She passed by in the middle of the night, wailing and crying out in a loud voice: 'My children, we must flee far away from this city!' At other times she cried: 'My children, where shall I take you?'" [Library of Congress - La Llorona: Roots, Branches, and the Missing Link from Spain](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2021/10/la-llorona-roots-branches-and-the-missing-link-from-spain/){evidence: "The people heard a weeping woman night after night. She passed by in the middle of the night, wailing and crying out in a loud voice: 'My children, we must flee far away from this city!' At other times she cried: 'My children, where shall I take you?'"}.

In some stories, an encounter with La Llorona is fatal — she sometimes appears in vehicles, warning against bad behavior before disappearing, similar to the vanishing hitchhiker archetype [Library of Congress - La Llorona: An Introduction](https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2021/10/la-llorona-an-introduction-to-the-weeping-woman/){evidence: "Sometimes she appears in your horse-drawn wagon or in your car, warning you against bad behavior, before disappearing, just like that other famous spirit, the vanishing hitchhiker. In some stories, an encounter with her is fatal."}.

### Philippine Aswang

The aswang is a shape-shifting supernatural creature from Philippine folklore — an umbrella term encompassing several types of malevolent entities including:

- **Manananggal** — a creature that separates its upper torso from its lower body and flies at night with bat-like wings, preying on pregnant women
- **Tik-tik** — named for the sound it makes, a variant that feeds on the unborn
- **Viscera sucker** — uses a proboscis-like tongue to feed on victims
- **Weredog/wereboar** — transforms into animals at night

Unlike Western ghosts, the aswang is not necessarily a spirit of the dead but a living person who transforms or is cursed. It occupies a unique position in paranormal taxonomy as a being that bridges the gap between living cursed humans and supernatural entities. The aswang represents a broader Southeast Asian tradition of **living ghosts** — people who become monstrous through curse, inheritance, or dark magic.

### Other Notable Cultural Spirits

- **Slavic Rusalka** — spirits of young women who died untimely deaths (often by drowning), who lure men to watery deaths
- **Scandinavian Draugr** — corporeal undead from Norse mythology who guard their burial mounds with superhuman strength
- **Indian Bhūt/Preta** — restless spirits of those who died violent or untimely deaths, often appearing with feet turned backward
- **Hawaiian Night Marchers (Huaka'i Pō)** — ghostly processions of ancient warriors who march on specific nights; eye contact is fatal
- **West African/Caribbean Duppy** — malicious spirits that can be summoned or sent to harm enemies through obeah (folk magic)
- **Thai Phi** — a vast taxonomy of spirits including Phi Tai Hong (violent death spirits), Phi Krasue (a floating head with dangling viscera), and Phi Pop (possession spirits)

## Summary

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Residual vs. Intelligent** remains the fundamental classification divide in paranormal research. Residual hauntings are environmental recordings with no consciousness; intelligent hauntings involve aware, responsive entities.

2. **The Stone Tape Theory** provides the most widely cited (though scientifically unvalidated) mechanism for residual hauntings — the idea that building materials can record and replay traumatic emotional energy.

3. **Crisis apparitions** are among the most evidentially interesting phenomena in psychical research, documented since 1882 by the Society for Psychical Research, involving the appearance of a person to loved ones at the precise moment of their death across distance.

4. **Shadow people and the Hat Man** represent a phenomenon sitting at the intersection of sleep science and paranormal belief — strongly correlated with sleep paralysis, substance use, and sleep deprivation, yet reported with remarkable consistency across cultures.

5. **Ghost vs. Spirit** is not merely semantic — it reflects a meaningful distinction between unconscious residual energy (ghost) and conscious, free-willed entities that have transitioned but can choose to return (spirit).

6. **Anniversary ghosts** represent a temporal variant of residual haunting, activating only on specific calendar dates tied to the original traumatic event.

7. **Cultural variations** reveal that while virtually every culture has ghost traditions, the specific forms differ dramatically — from Japan's revenge-driven onryō to China's karmic hungry ghosts, Ireland's prophetic banshee, Mexico's grief-stricken La Llorona, and the Philippines' living-dead aswang. These differences reflect each culture's unique relationship with death, morality, family obligation, and the afterlife.

8. **Orbs and ectoplasm** represent the most contested categories — orbs are now largely dismissed even by serious paranormal investigators as photographic artifacts, while ectoplasm has been almost entirely debunked as séance-room fraud, though both remain historically significant to the field's development.
