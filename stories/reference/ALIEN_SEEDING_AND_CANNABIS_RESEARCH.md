## 1.1 DIRECTED PANSPERMIA — The Nobel Laureate's Bombshell

### The Source
- **Who:** Francis Crick (co-discoverer of DNA's double helix structure) & Leslie Orgel (biochemist, Salk Institute)
- **What:** "Directed Panspermia" — a peer-reviewed scientific paper
- **When:** Published 1973 in *Icarus* (journal of astronomy and solar system studies, edited by Carl Sagan)
- **Where:** The paper is preserved in the National Library of Medicine archives (NIH)

### The Claim
Crick and Orgel proposed that life on Earth may have originated from microorganisms deliberately sent here by an advanced extraterrestrial civilization aboard unmanned spacecraft. This wasn't fringe rambling — it was a carefully argued paper by a Nobel Prize winner in the field's most respected journal.

Their reasoning:
- The universality of the genetic code (all Earth life uses the same DNA/RNA system) suggests a single origin event
- The speed at which life appeared on Earth after it became habitable seemed "too fast" for random chemistry
- An advanced civilization could have seeded multiple planets simultaneously as a form of "cosmic insurance" for their genetic heritage
- The spacecraft could carry frozen microorganisms protected from radiation during interstellar transit

### Key Quote from the Paper
> "As an alternative to these nineteenth-century mechanisms, we have considered Directed Panspermia, the theory that organisms were deliberately transmitted to the earth by intelligent beings on another planet."

### Later Developments
- In 1966, Shklovskii and Sagan had already speculated about directed panspermia
- Mautner and Matloff (1979) proposed the inverse — that *we* should seed other planets
- Richard Dawkins mentioned directed panspermia as plausible in the 2008 documentary *Expelled: No Intelligence Allowed*
- The concept remains scientifically legitimate — it's unfalsifiable but not irrational

## 2.1 THE FULL GROWTH CYCLE

### Stage 1: Germination (3-10 days)
- Seed absorbs water, swells, cracks open
- Taproot emerges first (white, fragile)
- Optimal conditions: 70-80°F (21-27°C), 70-80% humidity
- Methods: paper towel method, direct soil, water glass
- Seed should be planted taproot-down, 0.5-1 inch deep
- Light: not required until sprout breaks soil
- **Indicator of completion:** Two round cotyledon leaves emerge

### Stage 2: Seedling (2-3 weeks)
- First true leaves develop (single-bladed, then 3-bladed)
- Stem is thin, vulnerable
- Root system establishing
- Optimal conditions: 68-77°F, 65-70% humidity
- Light: 18-24 hours, low intensity (200-400 PPFD)
- Nutrients: NONE or extremely light (seedling-strength)
- Common errors: overwatering (most common killer), too much light

### Stage 3: Vegetative Growth (3-16 weeks)
- Rapid growth — stems thicken, leaves multiply, branches form
- Plants can grow 1-2 inches per day
- This is when plant structure is built
- **Photoperiod:** 18 hours light / 6 hours dark (most common)
- Some growers use 20/4 or even 24/0
- Optimal conditions: 70-85°F, 40-60% humidity
- Nutrients: HIGH nitrogen (N:P:K ratio ~3:1:2 or 4:2:1)
- Cal-mag supplementation critical (especially in coco coir)
- Training happens during this stage (LST, topping, etc.)
- **Indicator of readiness for flower:** Alternating nodes, pre-flowers visible

### Stage 4: Pre-Flower / Transition (1-2 weeks)
- Triggered by light change to 12/12
- "The stretch" — plants can double in height
- Sex becomes apparent (pistils = female, pollen sacs = male)
- Remove males immediately (unless breeding)
- Nutrients: transition from veg to bloom formula
- Trellis/support installation critical

### Stage 5: Flowering (8-11 weeks, strain dependent)
- **Early flower (weeks 1-3):** Stretch continues, first pistils, bud sites form
- **Mid flower (weeks 4-6):** Bud development, calyxes swell, trichomes appear
- **Late flower (weeks 7-9+):** Buds fatten, trichomes mature (clear → milky → amber)
- **Photoperiod:** 12 hours light / 12 hours UNINTERRUPTED dark
- Any light leak during dark period can cause hermaphroditism or revegging
- Optimal conditions: 65-80°F, 40-50% humidity (lower to prevent mold)
- Nutrients: HIGH phosphorus & potassium (N:P:K ratio ~1:3:2)
- Flush with plain water last 1-2 weeks (controversial but common practice)
- **Harvest indicator:** Trichome color via jeweler's loupe or microscope
  - Clear = too early (not fully mature)
  - Milky/cloudy = peak THC (energetic high)
  - Amber = THC degrading to CBN (sedative, couch-lock)
  - Most growers harvest at 70-80% milky, 20-30% amber

### Stage 6: Harvest & Drying (7-14 days)
- Cut plant (whole plant hang or individual branch hang)
- Dark room, 60°F, 60% relative humidity (the **60/60 rule**)
- Gentle indirect airflow (NOT pointed at buds)
- Duration: 7-14 days (slow dry = better quality)
- **Ready when:** Thin stems snap cleanly with a "crack" sound (not bend)
- Thick stems should still have slight flex
- Too fast = harsh smoke, lost terpenes
- Too slow = mold risk

### Stage 7: Curing (2-8 weeks minimum, up to 6 months for premium)
- Trim buds (wet trim vs. dry trim — dry trim preserves more trichomes)
- Place in wide-mouth glass mason jars (75% full)
- Target: 58-62% relative humidity inside jars (62% is the sweet spot)
- **Burping schedule:**
  - Week 1: Open jars 2-3 times daily for 10-15 minutes
  - Week 2: Once daily for 5-10 minutes
  - Week 3-4: Every 2-3 days
  - Week 5+: Weekly or as needed
- Use Boveda 62% or Integra Boost packs for humidity control
- Digital mini-hygrometers in each jar
- Store in dark, cool location (60-70°F)
- **What curing does:**
  - Breaks down chlorophyll (removes "grassy" taste)
  - Converts remaining starches and sugars
  - Stabilizes terpene profiles
  - Smooths smoke quality
  - Enhances potency perception

## 2.2 LIGHT SCIENCE — Spectrum & Photoperiod

### The Electromagnetic Menu
Cannabis responds to specific wavelengths differently:

| Wavelength | Color | Function |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| 280-315nm | UV-B | Increases trichome production (stress response) |
| 315-400nm | UV-A | Enhanced resin production |
| 400-500nm | Blue | Vegetative growth, compact nodes, strong stems |
| 500-600nm | Green | Penetrates canopy, minor photosynthesis contribution |
| 600-700nm | Red | Flowering trigger, photosynthesis peak, bud development |
| 700-750nm | Far-Red | Shade avoidance response, stretch, faster flowering |
| 730nm specific | Deep Far-Red | Phytochrome conversion, can accelerate flower transition |

### Photoperiod Science
- Cannabis is a **short-day plant** — it flowers when dark periods exceed ~12 hours
- The mechanism: phytochrome proteins (Pr and Pfr) act as light timers
  - Red light (660nm) converts Pr → Pfr (signals "day")
  - Far-red (730nm) converts Pfr → Pr (signals "night")
  - Pfr degrades during darkness — when it drops below threshold, flowering triggers
- **Any interruption of the dark period resets the clock** — even brief light exposure
- Some growers use a far-red "end of day" burst to accelerate Pfr conversion

### PAR, PPFD, and DLI
- **PAR** (Photosynthetically Active Radiation): 400-700nm range
- **PPFD** (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density): µmol/m²/s — the "intensity" measurement
  - Seedling: 200-400 PPFD
  - Vegetative: 400-600 PPFD

## 2.3 GROWING MEDIA — Soil vs. Hydro vs. Living Soil

### Soil (Simplest)
- Pre-mixed potting soils (Fox Farm Ocean Forest, Happy Frog, Roots Organics)
- Provides buffered nutrients for 2-4 weeks
- More forgiving of pH/nutrient errors
- Slower growth than hydro
- Natural microbial life aids nutrient absorption
- pH target: 6.2-6.8

### Coco Coir (Hydro-Soil Hybrid)
- Coconut husk fiber — inert medium
- Requires all nutrients added via fertigation
- Faster growth than soil, more forgiving than deep water culture
- Excellent drainage and oxygenation
- Requires frequent watering (1-3x daily when mature)
- MUST supplement calcium and magnesium (coco binds these)
- pH target: 5.5-6.5

### Deep Water Culture (DWC) — Pure Hydro
- Roots suspended in oxygenated nutrient solution
- Air pumps + air stones keep water oxygenated
- Fastest growth rates possible
- Most unforgiving — errors compound quickly
- Requires constant monitoring (pH, EC, temperature, dissolved oxygen)
- pH target: 5.5-6.0
- Water temperature: 65-68°F (too warm = root rot)

### Living Soil / No-Till (The "Craft" Method)
- Complete ecosystem in the pot: bacteria, fungi, insects, worms
- "Cook" soil before use (compost process activates biology)
- Minimal or no bottled nutrients — the soil feeds the plant
- Mycorrhizal networks deliver nutrients directly to roots
- Cover crops, mulch layers, worm castings
- Slowest initial growth, but **highest terpene expression** (generally)
- Considered the "wine terroir" approach to cannabis
- No pH adjustment needed once system is established
- Requires knowledge of soil biology, not just chemistry

## 2.6 CANNABIS GENETICS — Breeding & Selection

### Key Terminology
- **Genotype:** The plant's complete DNA blueprint (what it *can* express)
- **Phenotype:** The plant's observable traits (what it *does* express)
- **Same seeds, different phenos:** Even siblings can vary dramatically in height, aroma, potency, structure
- **Pheno hunting:** Growing many seeds of one cross and selecting the best expression
- **Cultivar:** The correct botanical term for "strain"

### Breeding Generations
- **P1 (Parent 1) × P2 (Parent 2):** The original cross
- **F1 (First Filial):** Direct offspring of P1 × P2. Highly vigorous (hybrid vigor) but variable
- **F2:** F1 × F1. Maximum phenotypic variation — the "pheno hunting" generation
- **F3, F4, F5...:** Each generation narrows variation with selection
- **BX (Backcross):** Crossing offspring back to a parent to lock in specific traits
- **IBL (Inbred Line):** A strain bred for many generations until nearly homozygous — every seed produces the same plant
- **S1:** Self-pollination (feminized seeds from a reversed female) — creates near-copies of mother

### What Breeders Do
1. Select P1 (mother) and P2 (father) for desired trait combination
2. Cross and grow F1 generation
3. Select best F1 individuals based on desired traits
4. Cross selected F1s to produce F2 (maximum variation)
5. Pheno hunt through F2 — grow 50-100+ and select the standouts
6. Backcross standouts to parents or cross siblings to stabilize
7. Repeat for 5-8 generations until strain is "stable"
8. A truly stable strain produces <10% variation between seeds

### The Feminization Process
- Regular seeds produce ~50/50 male/female ratio
- Feminized seeds produce 99%+ female plants
- Created by applying colloidal silver or silver thiosulfate (STS) to female flowers
- This forces female plants to produce pollen (genetically XX pollen)
- XX pollen + XX female = all-female offspring (feminized seeds)

## 2.7 ADVANCED TRAINING TECHNIQUES

### Low-Stress Training (LST)
- Gently bending branches and tying them down
- Creates flat, even canopy
- Exposes more bud sites to direct light
- No recovery time needed (plant isn't damaged)
- Start early in veg (flexible stems)
- Use garden wire, plant clips, or soft ties
- Retie every few days as plant grows

### Topping
- Cutting the main stem above a node
- Removes apical dominance — plant produces TWO main colas instead of one
- Creates bushier, wider plants
- Requires 3-7 days recovery
- Best done when plant has 4-6 nodes
- Can be repeated multiple times ("multiple topping")

### FIMming (Fuck I Missed)
- Pinching or cutting ~80% of new growth tip (leaving 20%)
- Less precise than topping but can produce 4+ new tops
- Faster recovery than topping
- More unpredictable results

### Mainlining / Manifolding
- Systematic topping to create perfectly symmetrical plant structure
- Top to 3rd node → strip everything below → let 2 branches grow → top each again
- Creates 8, 16, or 32 perfectly even cola sites
- Maximum uniformity — every bud gets equal light and nutrients
- Requires more time in veg but produces exhibition-quality plants
- The "bonsai" of cannabis growing

### SCROG (Screen of Green)
- Horizontal screen/net placed 12-18 inches above growing medium
- Plants grow through screen; branches woven through holes
- Creates perfectly flat canopy — all bud sites at same distance from light
- Maximum light efficiency
- Fewer plants needed (1-4 plants fill entire screen)
- Best combined with topping/LST
- Net typically made of string/twine in 2"×2" grid

### SOG (Sea of Green)
- MANY small plants in tight spacing
- Minimal or no veg time (flip to flower quickly)
- Each plant produces ONE main cola
- Fastest seed-to-harvest time
- Best with clones (uniform genetics)
- Requires more plants but less time per cycle
- Legal issues in some jurisdictions (plant count limits)

### Lollipopping
- Removing all growth from bottom 1/3 to 1/2 of plant
- "Lollipop" shape — bare stem on bottom, canopy on top
- Redirects energy from popcorn buds to main colas
- Improves airflow (reduces mold risk)
- Done at transition to flower or early flower
- Controversial — some argue lower buds still contribute sugar production

### Super Cropping
- Gently crushing/pinching main stem between fingers until inner fibers break (but outer skin stays intact)
- Branch folds 90° at crush point
- Creates "knuckle" that heals stronger — increasing nutrient flow capacity
- High-stress but high-reward
- Plant rebuilds stronger, thicker stem at crush point
- Do during veg only (not during flower)

## 2.9 CANNABINOID SCIENCE — How THC/CBD/CBG/CBN Form

### The Biosynthesis Pathway
All major cannabinoids start from the same precursor:

```
Olivetolic Acid + Geranyl Pyrophosphate
         ↓ (CBGAS enzyme)
    CBGA (Cannabigerolic Acid) — "The Mother Cannabinoid"
         ↓ ↓ ↓ (three different synthase enzymes)
    THCA    CBDA    CBCA
    (via THCAS) (via CBDAS) (via CBCAS)
         ↓ ↓ ↓ (heat/time — decarboxylation)
    THC     CBD     CBC
```

### Key Points
- **CBGA** is the precursor to ALL other cannabinoids — it's the stem cell
- **Synthase enzymes** (THCAS, CBDAS, CBCAS) determine which cannabinoid is produced
- **Genetics** determine which synthase enzymes are active (THC-dominant vs CBD-dominant)
- **All cannabinoids exist as ACIDS in the living plant** (THCA, CBDA, etc.)
- They become psychoactive only after **decarboxylation** (losing a CO2 molecule)

### Decarboxylation
- Occurs with heat (smoking, vaping, baking) or extended time/UV exposure
- THCA → THC + CO2 (temperature: ~104-120°C for 30-45 minutes)
- CBDA → CBD + CO2
- Raw cannabis is NOT psychoactive — THCA doesn't bind CB1 receptors effectively
- This is why you can't get high from eating raw flower

### CBN (Cannabinol) — The Degradation Product
- CBN doesn't come from CBGA directly
- It's what THC becomes when exposed to oxygen and UV light over time
- Old, improperly stored cannabis becomes CBN-rich
- Effects: Sedative, sleep-inducing (why old weed makes you sleepy but doesn't get you "high")
- Not produced by the plant — produced by degradation

### Trichome Development & Maturity
- Cannabinoids are produced EXCLUSIVELY in glandular trichomes
- Three types: bulbous (tiny), sessile (medium), capitate-stalked (largest, most productive)
- Capitate-stalked trichomes are visible as the "frosty" crystal coating
- Trichome maturity stages:
  - **Clear/transparent:** Immature. Cannabinoids still converting. Effects: racy, anxious
  - **Milky/cloudy:** Peak THC concentration. Effects: euphoric, energetic, cerebral
  - **Amber:** THC degrading to CBN. Effects: sedative, body-heavy, couch-lock
- Most growers harvest at **70-80% milky, 20-30% amber** for balanced effects

## 2.10 INDOOR vs. OUTDOOR vs. GREENHOUSE

### Indoor
**Advantages:**
- Total environmental control (temp, humidity, light, CO2)
- Year-round production (perpetual harvest possible)
- Privacy/security
- No pest pressure from environment

- Limited canopy size
- Equipment failure = crop loss
- Requires constant monitoring

### Outdoor
**Advantages:**
- Free light (the sun is unbeatable)
- Larger plants (10-15+ feet possible)
- Lower cost per gram
- Natural terpene development (UV + full spectrum sunlight)
- Massive yields per plant

**Disadvantages:**
- One harvest per year (photoperiod dependent)
- Weather vulnerability
- Pest/disease pressure
- Less control over final product
- Visible (security concerns)

### Greenhouse (Light Deprivation / "Light Dep")
**Advantages:**
- Natural sunlight + environmental protection
- Can force flowering with blackout tarps (multiple harvests per year)
- Lower energy cost than full indoor
- Excellent light quality (sunlight + supplemental LEDs)
- Scalable for commercial operations

**Disadvantages:**
- Initial infrastructure cost
- Temperature management challenging (can overheat)
- Still some pest pressure
- Requires land/space
