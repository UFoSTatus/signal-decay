# PART I: RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS

## 1. Synesthesia & Cross-Modal Perception

### What Science Tells Us

Synesthesia is the involuntary association of different senses — where stimulation in one modality produces genuine perceptual experience in another. Research from Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (2014) demonstrates that this isn't metaphorical: synesthetes show measurably different N1 ERP components during cross-modal perception, meaning the brain genuinely processes the secondary experience as real sensory input.

**Key findings relevant to Signal Decay:**

- **Grapheme-color synesthesia** is the most common form (86% of synesthetes). Letters and numbers consistently evoke specific colors. This is *involuntary* — the synesthete cannot choose not to see the color.
- **Music-color synesthesia** occurs in 41% of synesthetes. Sound literally becomes visual.
- **Lexical-gustatory synesthesia** — certain words produce actual taste sensations.
- Critically: **synesthetes show enhanced memory** because the extra sensory channel provides additional encoding pathways. Grapheme-color synesthetes have measurably superior recall.
- Research shows that even non-synesthetes have latent cross-modal correspondences (Spence, 2011): high pitches feel "bright" and "sharp," low pitches feel "dark" and "round." This is universal.

### Design Implications

We don't need to give users clinical synesthesia. We need to **activate the latent cross-modal mappings that already exist in every human brain.** If we pair frequency information (our audio layer) with visual/textual changes using the *right* correspondences, readers will experience something that feels synesthetic — a blurring of the boundary between hearing the story and seeing it, between reading text and sensing frequency.

The key insight: **The correspondences must be consistent but never explained.** A synesthete doesn't learn that "A" is red — they just perceive it. Our system must create the same kind of involuntary, consistent, unexplained associations.

## 2. Neuroscience of Memory Recall & Recovery

### Voluntary vs. Involuntary Memory

A 2025 PLOS Biology study (PMC12367169) demonstrates something remarkable: **involuntary and voluntary memory recall rely on DISTINCT neural processes.** They are not the same mechanism at different intensities — they are fundamentally different brain operations.

**Voluntary recall:** Intentional search → hippocampal pattern completion → sequential assembly of details. Feels effortful.

**Involuntary recall:** Cue triggers → spontaneous reactivation of entire engram → floods consciousness with vivid, emotional, complete memory. Feels overwhelming and unbidden.

The **Proust Phenomenon** (madeleine episode) is the classic example: a sensory cue (taste, smell, sound) bypasses conscious memory search entirely and detonates a complete, vivid autobiographical memory. The key is that the recall is:
- Triggered by a **specific sensory cue** (not a thought)
- **Emotionally charged** (feels more real than voluntary recall)
- **Holistic** — the entire memory arrives at once, not piece by piece
- **Surprising** — the rememberer didn't know they had this memory

### Engram Theory

Memory engram research (Josselyn & Tonegawa, Nature Reviews Neuroscience) shows that memories are stored in sparse ensembles of neurons called **engram cells.** These cells are:
- Activated during encoding
- **Reactivated** during recall (same cells fire again)
- Modified during **reconsolidation** (each recall subtly alters the memory)
- Subject to **pattern completion** — partial cue activates entire pattern

**Critical for Signal Decay:** Memory reconsolidation means every act of remembering *changes* the memory. This is not a bug — it's how memory works. Our system should model this: each time a reader encounters a memory fragment, the fragment should subtly shift, growing richer or more distorted depending on the context of recall.

### Design Implications

We must simulate the **feeling** of involuntary memory recovery:
1. The trigger must be sensory (audio, visual motion, haptic) not textual instruction
2. The memory must arrive *whole* — not revealed word by word
3. It must feel like it was always there, just below the surface
4. Each subsequent encounter should modify the memory (reconsolidation)
5. The emotional charge must precede the content (you feel it before you know it)

## 3. Music Theory as Puzzle Language

### The Harmonic Series

The harmonic series is the foundation of all musical consonance. When a string vibrates at frequency f (fundamental), it simultaneously produces overtones at 2f, 3f, 4f, 5f... These are not separate notes — they are inherent properties of the single vibrating system.

**Sympathetic resonance** occurs when an undamped string vibrates in response to another string sounding its fundamental or overtone. In a piano, if you hold down the sustain pedal and sing a note, the piano sings back — the strings whose frequencies match your voice's harmonics vibrate in sympathy.

**Key concepts for puzzle design:**

- **Consonance** = simple frequency ratios (octave = 2:1, fifth = 3:2, fourth = 4:3). These feel resolved, complete, stable.
- **Dissonance** = complex frequency ratios (minor second = 16:15, tritone = 45:32). These feel tense, unresolved, demanding resolution.
- **Beating** = when two frequencies are close but not identical, they produce a pulsing effect (amplitude modulation). The beat frequency = |f1 - f2|.
- **Resonance** = when an external force matches a system's natural frequency, the system's amplitude increases dramatically.
- **Interference** = two waves combine constructively (in phase = louder) or destructively (out of phase = silence).

### The Octave Model Connection

Our existing octave model (physical → entity → cosmic) maps perfectly to the harmonic series:
- **First Octave (physical)** = fundamental frequency. The grounded reality.
- **Second Octave (entity)** = first overtone (octave above). Same "note" but in a higher register — same events viewed from entity consciousness.
- **Third Octave (cosmic)** = higher harmonics. The pattern that connects everything.

This isn't metaphor — it's structural. The relationship between octaves in Signal Decay IS the relationship between harmonics in physics. They are the same mathematics.

### Design Implications

Musical relationships can encode puzzle logic:
- Two story elements that are "consonant" (related) literally sound consonant together
- Unresolved plot threads are literally dissonant frequencies demanding resolution
- Characters whose stories overlap share harmonic frequencies (sympathetic resonance)
- The "answer" to a narrative puzzle is the frequency that resolves the dissonance
- Interference patterns between overlapping stories create beats — temporal pulses that mark important moments

## 4. Radio Tuning & Signal Scanning

### How Tuning Works

A radio receiver is a **resonant circuit** — an LC (inductor-capacitor) circuit that responds strongly at ONE frequency and rejects all others. Tuning = adjusting the resonant frequency to match the desired signal.

**The experience of tuning a radio:**
1. Static (noise floor — all frequencies at once, no signal)
2. Approach — signal emerges from static, faint, distorted
3. Lock — suddenly clear, stable, resolved
4. Overshoot — signal fades, distortion returns
5. Re-tune — minute adjustment back to clarity

**Shortwave radio** adds another dimension: **skywave propagation.** Signals bounce off the ionosphere and arrive from impossible distances, fading in and out as atmospheric conditions change. The same frequency can carry different content at different times of day. Signals **bleed** into each other at adjacent frequencies.

### Design Implications

The tuning metaphor is perfect for Signal Decay because:
- It's an analog, continuous process (not binary on/off)
- The signal was always there — you just couldn't hear it
- Skill develops through practice (experienced listeners know what "almost tuned" sounds like)
- Adjacent frequencies interfere — you might catch fragments of other signals while seeking one
- Atmospheric conditions change — the same "frequency" might reveal different content at different times
- The act of listening IS the act of finding

## 7. Biofeedback & Reading Behavior as Input

### Reading as Measurable Behavior

Without any hardware sensors, a web/mobile app can detect:
- **Scroll speed** — how fast the reader moves through text
- **Dwell time** — how long they spend on each paragraph/sentence
- **Re-reading patterns** — scrolling back up (the reader is confused, or searching)
- **Touch patterns** — where they tap, long-press, how they hold the phone
- **Session timing** — time of day, duration, frequency of sessions
- **Pause patterns** — stopping mid-text (distraction? deep thought? emotional reaction?)

Eye-tracking research (University of Minnesota, 2023) shows that reader engagement correlates with specific measurable behaviors: longer fixation times on engaging passages, increased re-reading of complex sections, slower progression through emotionally charged content.

### Design Implications

We can use reading behavior as **implicit input** without any explicit game actions:
- Lingering on a passage = interest signal → reveal more
- Re-reading = confusion or fascination → disambiguate or deepen
- Speed-reading = low engagement → hold back reveals
- Pausing after a specific sentence = potential emotional resonance → mark as significant
- Reading at 3 AM = altered state → present different content than daytime reading

The reader never "plays" — they just read. But HOW they read is the input.

## 10. Quantum Observation & The Act of Looking

### The Observer Effect

In quantum mechanics, the act of measurement fundamentally changes the system being measured. Before observation, a quantum system exists in superposition — multiple states simultaneously. Observation "collapses" the wave function into a single definite state.

The **double-slit experiment** demonstrates this most clearly: electrons behave as waves when unobserved but as particles when observed. The observer doesn't just reveal the state — the observer DETERMINES it.

**Von Neumann-Wigner interpretation** goes further: consciousness itself is what collapses the wave function. The act of conscious observation creates definite reality from quantum possibility.

### Design Implications

What if reading a passage **actually changes it?**

Not metaphorically. Not "the story branches." The literal text changes based on whether (and how) it has been observed:
- A passage that hasn't been read exists in "superposition" — multiple potential versions
- The first reading "collapses" it into a specific version (influenced by reader state)
- Re-reading may reveal the *same* text has shifted (the observation changed it)
- Passages you've never read remain in all potential states until you look

This violates the reader's fundamental assumption: that text is stable, that a book is the same each time you open it. Breaking this assumption is DEEPLY unsettling in exactly the way Signal Decay needs to be.

## 12. Wave Interference & Phase Relationships

### Constructive and Destructive Interference

When two waves meet:
- **In phase** (crests aligned) → constructive interference → doubled amplitude
- **Out of phase** (crest meets trough) → destructive interference → silence/cancellation
- **Partial alignment** → complex interference pattern with areas of reinforcement and cancellation

**Standing waves** form when interference creates fixed nodes (always silent) and antinodes (always loud). The pattern is stable but created by two moving waves.

**Beating** occurs when frequencies are close but not identical: the combined signal oscillates between loud and quiet at a rate equal to the frequency difference.

### Design Implications

If stories are waves, and characters are frequencies:
- Two stories that are **in phase** (thematically aligned) reinforce each other → connections become obvious
- Two stories **out of phase** contradict each other → information cancels → hidden content becomes visible in the gaps
- Stories that are nearly-but-not-quite aligned **beat** — creating a rhythmic pulsing of insight/confusion
- **Standing wave patterns** in the narrative create fixed points of clarity and fixed points of mystery

The reader navigating multiple stories is experiencing an interference pattern. Their job is to find the **nodes** (fixed points of truth) and the **antinodes** (points of maximum uncertainty) and understand what creates each.

# PART II: MECHANIC INVENTION

## The Overarching Concept: FREQUENCY READING

All proposed mechanics share a single philosophy: **reading is not a visual act — it is an act of tuning.** The reader is not consuming text on a page. They are adjusting their perceptual frequency to receive signals that exist in the narrative space. Some signals are strong (surface text, obvious plot). Some are weak (hidden patterns, suppressed memories, entity whispers). The reader's "tuning" gets better over time as they develop sensitivity to weaker signals.

The system tracks this not through points or levels but through a **resonance state** — a complex number representing the reader's current alignment with each story's frequency signature. High resonance = the story reveals more. Low resonance = the story remains opaque. Resonance develops organically through reading behavior, pattern recognition, and cross-story connections.

## MECHANIC 1: PHASE DRIFT

### Name: Phase Drift

### User Experience

You're reading a story. The background audio is a gentle ambient drone — you barely notice it. As you read, certain paragraphs feel... different. Not in any way you can point to at first. The text is the same. The font is the same. But something is off.

Then you notice it: the audio has shifted. Not dramatically — you couldn't say when it changed. But the frequency is slightly different now. There's a subtle beating between the ambient sound and... something else. A second frequency, barely perceptible, creating a slow pulse.

You keep reading. The beating gets faster as you approach certain sentences. At one specific sentence, the beating stops entirely — perfect phase alignment. And in that moment of silence/clarity, a single word in the text subtly shifts weight. Not changes — it was always that word — but it MEANS differently now. It connects to something you read three stories ago. You feel it before you think it.

If you scroll back, the beating is gone. The moment has passed. You can't force it. But you've learned something: the audio is telling you something the text isn't.

Over sessions, you develop an instinct. You learn to read with your ears as much as your eyes. You learn that the phase drift isn't random — it's pointing you toward connections between stories, between characters, between moments. You start seeking the alignment points. You start reading like a radio operator scanning frequencies.

### Technical Implementation

```
AUDIO LAYER:
- Base: Procedural ambient drone at frequency F_story (unique per story)
- Hidden: Second oscillator at F_related (frequency of a RELATED story's theme)
- Phase offset: Varies based on text position via IntersectionObserver
- Beat frequency: |F_story - F_related| * position_coefficient
- At "resonance points" (tagged in content): phase offset = 0 → beat disappears

DOM LAYER:
- IntersectionObserver tracks which paragraph is in viewport center
- Each paragraph has a phase_distance value (authored metadata)
- As phase_distance approaches 0, audio layers align
- At alignment: CSS custom property --resonance triggers subtle text effect
  (letter-spacing shift of 0.02em, opacity micro-pulse, font-weight 399→401)

DETECTION:
- No UI indicator. Pure audio + near-subliminal visual.
- Reader develops sensitivity over time.
- System tracks dwell_time at resonance points as engagement metric.

STATE:
- Each resonance point "discovered" (lingered on during alignment) is logged
- Discovered points unlock cross-story connections on Investigation Board
- Multiple discoveries in same session increase global resonance_state
- Higher resonance_state causes future phase_distances to be shorter (easier)
```

### Why It's Original

No existing game uses audio phase alignment as a navigation mechanism for text. Her Story uses keyword search. Obra Dinn uses visual deduction. Outer Wilds uses time loops. This uses the PHYSICS OF WAVES as a literal mechanic: the reader finds truth where frequencies align. It's not a game metaphor plastered onto reading — it IS the physics of the story's world manifested as interaction.

### Integration with Reading

It doesn't interrupt reading AT ALL. The audio plays continuously. The text is readable regardless. The mechanic exists as an additional perceptual layer that rewards attention but punishes nothing.

### Memory Recovery Connection

Each resonance point corresponds to a suppressed memory fragment. Finding it (lingering during phase alignment) literally "tunes" the reader to that memory's frequency, making it accessible in the character chat system. Characters might say: "You're asking about [topic]? I... I remember feeling something about that. Like hearing a note I'd forgotten existed."

### Feasibility: 9/10
All components exist in Web Audio API + IntersectionObserver + CSS custom properties. The authoring requirement is moderate (tagging resonance points in stories).

### Wow Factor: 7/10
Subtle. Builds slowly. Experienced readers will find it profound. New readers might miss it entirely for sessions.

## MECHANIC 2: ENGRAM BLEEDING

### Name: Engram Bleeding

### User Experience

You're reading Yuki Tanaka's account of the signal detection at Hessdalen. Standard narrative — tense, atmospheric, her voice coming through clearly. Then mid-paragraph, for just a fraction of a second, a word... wasn't Yuki's. It was someone else's vocabulary. Someone else's syntax pattern.

You re-read the paragraph. Everything looks normal. But you have a nagging feeling. You read on.

Three paragraphs later, it happens again — but this time you catch it. The sentence is: "The oscilloscope showed nothing but I *knew* the signal was real, the way you know your own name." That italicized phrase. That's not Yuki. That's how Marcus Williams talks. That's his verbal tic — that specific comparison structure.

Now you're alert. You keep reading. And you realize: this isn't a glitch. Yuki's narrative is being INFILTRATED by Marcus's voice. As if his memories are bleeding into hers. As if the boundary between their experiences is breaking down.

You check the text more carefully. It IS Yuki's story. But embedded in it, like a null cipher, are fragments of Marcus's perspective. Sentence fragments. Word choices. Emotional colorings. The more you look, the more you find. And each fragment you identify reveals a new data point: where was Marcus when Yuki had this experience? What was he seeing from his perspective? The bleeding goes both ways.

On your Investigation Board, you place these fragments. And suddenly a pattern emerges: Yuki and Marcus were at the same event but remember it differently. The interference between their memories reveals what ACTUALLY happened — something neither fully remembers alone.

### Technical Implementation

```
CONTENT AUTHORING:
- Each character has a defined "voice signature" (vocabulary set, syntax patterns,
  typical metaphors, sentence length distribution, emotional markers)
- Stories authored with intentional "bleeds" — sentences where another character's
  voice signature replaces the narrator's at specific syntactic positions
- Bleeds are tagged with source_character and bleed_intensity metadata
- Bleeds escalate: word → phrase → sentence → paragraph over the course of a story

DOM MANIPULATION:
- Bleed text rendered identically to normal text (no visual distinction initially)
- Reader's resonance_state with BOTH characters determines bleed visibility:
  - Low resonance with both → bleeds invisible (rendered but styled identically)
  - High resonance with narrator → bleeds become subtly different (micro-typography)
  - High resonance with BOTH characters → bleeds visually distinct (color shift, 
    voice tag appears on hover/long-press)
  
PROGRESSION:
- First encounter: Reader notices "something off" but can't identify it
- After reading multiple stories from both characters: Bleeds become detectable
- After identifying several bleeds: System reveals that this is a mechanic
  (NOT through tutorial — through character dialog: "Do my words sound like
  someone else's to you? I've been hearing her voice in my head...")
- Late game: Reader can identify bleed sources instantly, mapping connections

AUDIO INTEGRATION:
- During bleed passages, the ambient audio subtly shifts to the OTHER character's
  frequency signature (each character has a unique timbral/frequency identity)
- The shift is proportional to bleed_intensity
- Sharp readers will hear the character change before seeing it in text

AI GENERATION (Azure):
- AI can generate additional bleeds for procedural stories using character voice models
- AI can analyze reader-submitted "I think this is a bleed" guesses and confirm/deny
- Character chat naturally exhibits bleeds when discussing related memories
```

### Why It's Original

No narrative game has used **cross-character voice contamination** as a puzzle mechanic. The closest analog is an unreliable narrator, but this is different: the narrator IS reliable — it's their MEMORY that's been invaded by another consciousness. The reader doesn't just discover "what happened" — they discover that the boundary between people's experiences is not as solid as assumed. This is thematically perfect for a story about entities and frequencies and consciousness.

### Integration with Reading

Completely integrated. The bleeds ARE the text. You can read every story straight through and enjoy them as narratives. The bleed detection is a layer of meaning that emerges only when you've read enough from multiple characters to recognize their voices. It rewards CLOSE reading and cross-story familiarity.

### Memory Recovery Connection

Each bleed represents a **shared memory** — a moment where two characters' experiences overlapped. Identifying the bleed unlocks the INTERSECTION memory: the scene as experienced from both perspectives simultaneously. These intersection memories are the most important ones for understanding the core mystery because they show what the entities were doing at moments when multiple witnesses were present.

### Feasibility: 8/10
Voice signatures can be defined with NLP metrics. DOM manipulation is trivial. The authoring load is significant (careful craft of bleeds that read naturally in context) but not technically complex. AI assistance for generation and detection is straightforward.

### Wow Factor: 9/10
The first time a reader realizes they're hearing another character's voice embedded in the narrator's text — and that this has been happening throughout stories they've already read — is a genuinely unprecedented narrative experience.

You finish reading a passage about Yuki detecting an anomalous signal at 7.83 Hz. As you read the frequency value, your phone vibrates — not a notification buzz, something subtler. A pulse. At 7.83 Hz? No — you can't feel 7.83 Hz. But it was... rhythmic. Strange.

Later, in a completely different story — Elara Moonwhisper discussing ancient ley lines — a sentence mentions "the pulse of the earth's own heartbeat." Your phone pulses again. Same rhythm. You didn't do anything. You just read.

The Investigation Board now shows a new connection available: "7.83 Hz — Schumann Resonance." You didn't create this connection. The app created it because you encountered BOTH references. But here's the thing — two other readers who read the same stories in different order got different connection patterns. Because resonance isn't just about WHAT you encounter — it's about the ORDER and TIMING of encounters.

You start exploring. You read more stories. Each time a frequency, a pattern, a phenomenon appears that connects to something you've previously encountered, the app maps it. But not as a simple list of links. As a **waveform interference pattern.** On the Investigation Board, your discoveries are arranged not as pins-and-string but as overlapping wave circles — like ripples in a pond. Where ripples intersect (constructive interference), the content is crystal clear. Where they cancel (destructive interference), there are gaps — things you should be able to see but can't yet.

The gaps ARE the remaining puzzles. You can see their shape by the pattern of what surrounds them. Like hearing the outline of a melody by the notes around it.

  - Which frequency_signatures they've encountered

## MECHANIC 7: TEMPORAL INTERFERENCE

### Name: Temporal Interference

### User Experience

You've read the same story from Yuki three times now. Not because you were told to — because something keeps drawing you back. Each time, the story is... the same. The same words. The same events. You're sure of it.

Except.

On your third read, you notice a sentence you swear wasn't there before. Not one you missed — one that WASN'T THERE. "The shadow on the wall moved against the light." You'd remember that. That's vivid. That's startling. That sentence did not exist the first two times you read this story.

You check: is the app showing you something new? You look at the modification date. Nothing's changed. You open the story in a different context (Investigation Board reference). The sentence is there. It's always been there. Hasn't it?

But then — a sentence that WAS there is gone. You remember it clearly: "The monitor beeped three times in quick succession." It's not there now. The paragraph flows without it. It works without it. But you KNOW it was there.

What's happening: the story exists in multiple temporal versions that the app subtly rotates through based on your reading count, time elapsed, and resonance state. Changes are never dramatic — a sentence here, a phrase there, a slight reframing of an event. Just enough to make you question your own memory. Just enough to blur the line between "the text changed" and "I misremembered."

And that IS the point. The characters experience this exact phenomenon — memories that shift, details that appear and disappear, the inability to distinguish between change and misremembering. You're not reading about unreliable memory. You're EXPERIENCING it.

### Technical Implementation

```
TEMPORAL VERSIONING SYSTEM:
- Each story maintains N "temporal layers" (typically 3-5)
- Layers differ by: inserted sentences, removed sentences, word substitutions,
  paragraph reorderings (subtle), perspective shifts (tiny)
- Differences are SMALL — never plot-changing, never contradictory to the 
  core narrative. Atmospheric details. Specific observations. Peripheral events.
  
LAYER SELECTION:
- Active layer determined by compound function:
  layer_index = hash(
    reader_id,
    story_id,
    read_count,
    days_since_first_read,
    resonance_state_at_time_of_access,
    time_of_day
  ) % num_layers
  
- This ensures:
  - Same reader, same story, different time → potentially different layer
  - Same reader re-reading within minutes → same layer (no obvious change)
  - Same reader after days → different layer (memory has decayed enough 
    that small changes are deniable)
  - Different readers → different layers (no two readers can confirm each other)

CHANGE CHARACTERISTICS:
- Added sentences: Vivid, memorable details that feel like they should have
  been noticed. Creates "I WOULD have remembered that" sensation.
- Removed sentences: Specific details replaced by more generic phrasing.
  Creates "I remember it being more specific" sensation.
- Word substitutions: Synonym swaps that shift emotional coloring.
  Creates "I thought the tone was different" sensation.
- Never: Character names, core plot events, dialogue attribution, 
  factual elements that reader might have written down.

GASLIGHTING PREVENTION:
- Reader's FIRST read of a story is recorded with full text snapshot
- Investigation Board shows their "first encounter" version as reference
- But the Investigation Board entry itself may show subtle differences
  from their actual first-read (because memory of the Investigation Board
  is ALSO subject to temporal interference)
- The system never DENIES change — it simply makes change undistinguishable
  from reader error

THEMATIC INTEGRATION:
- The temporal layers correspond to octave perspectives:
  - Layer shifts ADD second/third octave details on later reads
  - What appears to be "text that wasn't there" is actually higher-octave
    content becoming visible as reader's resonance state increases
  - The "removal" of text is lower-octave content becoming irrelevant
    as reader's understanding deepens
- This means the temporal interference ISN'T arbitrary — it's the story
  revealing more of itself to a reader who's ready to perceive it
```

### Why It's Original

No narrative system has implemented **deniable text mutation** — changes so subtle and so infrequent that the reader cannot distinguish between "the text changed" and "I misremembered." Choose Your Own Adventure changes text based on explicit choices. This changes text based on NOTHING the reader did — just time passing and knowledge accumulating. The reader cannot "solve" the changes because they can never be certain they're happening. This creates a philosophical unease that mirrors the characters' experience perfectly.

### Integration with Reading

Completely transparent. The reader just reads. Changes are only perceivable across multiple readings separated by time. No UI, no indicators, no acknowledgment from the system that anything is different. The reader must decide FOR THEMSELVES whether the text is changing or their memory is unreliable.

### Memory Recovery Connection

The temporal interference IS the memory recovery system in miniature. Characters in Signal Decay have memories that shift each time they're recalled (reconsolidation). The reader experiences this same phenomenon with the stories themselves. When a reader finally discusses a "changed" passage with a character in chat, the character might say: "I remember it that way too. Or... wait. Did I? It's hard to hold on to. Each time I try to remember, it's slightly different."

### Feasibility: 9/10
Trivial to implement technically — it's just serving different text versions with a deterministic hash. The authoring load is moderate (writing 3-5 subtle variants per story). No runtime computation beyond hash calculation. Works fully offline. The difficulty is EDITORIAL: the variants must be subtle enough to be deniable but significant enough to be noticeable.

### Wow Factor: 8/10
The existential unease of "is the text changing or am I misremembering?" is profoundly unsettling in a way no game has achieved. But it's slow — the wow moment takes multiple readings over days/weeks to manifest.

## DETAILED DESIGN 3: FREQUENCY ARCHAEOLOGY

### User Flow (Step by Step)

**The Discovery (First Encounter)**
1. Reader is deep in a story. Background ambient audio has been consistent for 10+ minutes.
2. At an authored silence point (dramatic pause in narrative), the ambient fades.
3. Fade takes 2 seconds. Not jarring — feels like the story taking a breath.
4. In the silence, the reader's ears adjust. They notice: it's not actually silent.
5. A faint, pure tone persists. Like tinnitus. Like a frequency that was always there under the music.
6. If on headphones: the tone seems to exist INSIDE their head (mono, center-panned, no spatial cues — mimics neural signal rather than external sound).
7. The reader might think it's their own tinnitus. That's intentional.

**The Scroll Discovery**
8. Reader scrolls — either intentionally or to continue reading
9. The tone CHANGES. Not dramatically. But perceptibly (±2Hz shift)
10. Reader pauses. Scrolls again. Yes — the tone tracks scroll position.
11. Realization dawns: the tone is responding to WHERE I am in the document
12. Reader begins experimentally scrolling. Slowly. Listening.
13. At certain positions, the tone strengthens (amplitude increases)
14. At other positions, additional harmonics appear (more complex signal)
15. One position produces a clear peak: loud(er), rich harmonics, beating stops

**The Excavation**
16. At the peak position, the reader's visual attention is drawn to the space between paragraphs — the white gap where the signal is strongest
17. Reader taps/long-presses the gap (or on mobile: two-finger tap)
18. The screen transitions:
    - Text above and below fades to 20% opacity
    - The gap expands vertically (100px → 300px animation, 500ms)
    - A spectrogram visualization appears in the expanded space
    - The hidden audio signal is rendered visually in real-time
    - The spectrogram reveals: text characters forming in the frequency space
19. Over 5-10 seconds, the spectral image resolves:
    - First: abstract swirls (the reader sees "something" but can't read it)
    - Then: letter forms emerge from the frequency patterns
    - Finally: readable text crystallizes
    - "She heard it first at seven. It followed her home from school. 
       It lived in the walls. Mother said it was the pipes."
20. The excavated memory fragment is captured:
    - Text solidifies and becomes permanent in the expanded gap
    - Color shifts from spectral rainbow to character's signature color
    - A gentle "recording found" audio cue plays (analog tape deck click)
    - Fragment is automatically added to Investigation Board

**Returning to Reading**
21. After excavation, the gap contracts back to normal size
22. But a tiny indicator remains: a barely-visible frequency icon (≈) in the margin
23. The ambient audio resumes, now enriched with the excavated frequency
24. Reading continues. The story feels different now — the excavated memory adds context
25. Characters may reference the excavation in subsequent chat:
    "You found the one buried in Hessdalen, didn't you? Between the paragraphs. 
     I put it there — or something put it there through me. I don't remember 
     writing it but I remember feeling it."

**Progressive Difficulty**
26. Early dig sites: obvious (long silence, clear tone, strong signal)
27. Mid dig sites: subtle (brief silence, quiet tone, reader must be attentive)
28. Late dig sites: hidden (no silence — reader must notice tonal anomaly in active ambient, minimal signal until resonance_state is high enough to "amplify" it)
29. Expert dig sites: require SPECIFIC reading state (must have identified certain bleeds OR reached certain resonance level before the site "activates")
