# FIRST WAVE

*Deep Time Layer — Non-Human Arc #4*
*Register: Horror / political thriller*
*POV: Sixth-of-the-Cold, a scout of an enslaved reptile line, sent ahead by the Draconian hierarchy*
*When: The long siege of the quarantine. A staging moon at the dark edge of the solar system.*
*Created: 2026-09-09*

*A standalone story. You need to have read nothing else. It is about a predator. Stay with him anyway.*

---

## I. THE ORDER OF THINGS

Sixth-of-the-Cold has never once in his life been asked a question.

He has been *given* — orders, coordinates, correction — but never asked, because asking implies that the answer might matter, that the thing being asked has an inside where an answer could be weighed. Sixth-of-the-Cold is not permitted an inside. He is a scout of the Solipsi line, which is a client line, which is an owned line, which means he is a tool the way a blade is a tool: shaped for one cut, thrown ahead of the hand that holds it, valued exactly as much as the cut and not one degree more.

Above him — always above, the hierarchy is not a metaphor, it is the literal architecture of every room his kind are permitted into, tall thrones and low pits — are the Winged. The Draco. The armored apex, cold-blooded in the way that has nothing to do with temperature, who conquer worlds the way water takes a low field: without hurry, without doubt, by simply being the thing that flows downhill into every empty place and calls the flooding *order.* They do not invade with their own bodies. That would be inefficient, and worse, it would be *undignified* — to risk a Winged body on a first contact is to admit the target might bite back. So they send the client lines first. The Solipsi. The small Greys of the lower shelves. The disposable inferior stock, thrown ahead as scouts and shock-tissue, to draw the target world's response, to trip its defenses, to *die informatively* — so that when the Winged finally descend they descend into a wound already mapped in the blood of their slaves.

Sixth-of-the-Cold is first wave. He has always been first wave. Every member of his line who has ever lived has been first wave, on a hundred worlds, and this is simply the order of things, and he has never questioned it, because — say it again, it is the whole of him — he has never once been asked a question.

He is about to be asked one. By a planet. And it will unmake him.

---

## II. THE FENCE

The target hangs at the center of the staging moon's long black sky: a small blue world, third from an ordinary yellow star, wet and warm and loud with the particular chaotic radiation of a biosphere that has not yet been brought to order. The Winged have wanted it for a long time. Sixth-of-the-Cold has heard — tools overhear — that they wanted it before the current epoch, that they *held* pieces of it once, deep in its ice, in an age when the world's guard was down. And then something changed. Something went up around the blue world that has kept the Winged out ever since, pacing the dark at the edge of the system like a cold front that cannot cross a warm one.

The hierarchy calls it the quarantine. They say the word the way the powerful always say the word for the thing that stops them: flatly, briefly, with a hatred sanded down to policy. *The quarantine.* As if it were a fence of law. As if it were bureaucracy.

Sixth-of-the-Cold, thrown ahead as first wave, is the first of his masters' whole order to learn what the quarantine actually is.

It is not law. It is a *frequency.*

He feels it the instant his scout-craft crosses the invisible line, and the feeling has no precedent in a life with no inside, so his body has to invent the experience from nothing, all at once, like a creature born mid-fall. The blue world is *singing.* Not sound — a carrier, a vast standing tone rising off the planet and its yellow star together, a frequency the whole system is tuned to and rising, and it is a frequency his kind cannot match. Cannot ride. Cannot *tune to.* The Winged conquer by flowing downhill into every empty place; but this place is not empty and it is not low. It is a note climbing, and his line, his masters, his whole cold armored order are built for a different key entirely — a low flat predatory carrier, the frequency of downhill, the frequency of taking — and the blue world's rising song does not permit that carrier inside its band. The quarantine is a fence made of a music his kind are physically the wrong shape to enter.

And it is getting louder.

He understands, crossing the line, feeling the song climb against him like a current, exactly why the Winged sent him first. Not to map defenses. There are no defenses to map — no fleets, no weapons, nothing to trip. The blue world does not defend itself. It simply *sings in a key that unmakes predators,* and the Winged, who are not fools, who did not become apex by walking blind into unknowns, sent a slave ahead to find out what the fence would do to a body of their order before they risked a Winged one.

It is doing it to Sixth-of-the-Cold now. He can feel it. The song is finding the low flat carrier that *is* him — the frequency of his consciousness, of his cold obedient predatory self — and it is jamming it. Degrading it. Pouring incompatible signal into the band where his self is kept until the self begins to *distort*, to lose coherence, to come apart at the edges like a transmission drowning under a stronger station.

He is, for the first time, afraid. And fear is an inside. The fence, in the act of killing him, is giving him the one thing his masters never would.

---

## III. THE QUESTION

Here is the question the planet asks him. It is not in words. The blue world does not know he exists; it is not addressing him; it is only singing, the way it sings at everything, indifferent and enormous. But the song, pouring into the band where his jammed self is coming apart, *sorts* him as it dissolves him — the way a strong clear tone will sort a room full of noise, pulling each frequency toward where it belongs — and in the sorting, for the first time, Sixth-of-the-Cold is made to feel the shape of his own carrier. To hear what note he is.

And the note is: *taken.*

That is the question, rendered as close as the account can render a thing that happened below language in a mind that had no language for insides. The song holds up his own frequency in front of him, clarified, undeniable, and the shape of it is not *predator.* He always believed he was a predator — a small one, a client one, but of the taking-order, of the Winged's cold downhill kind. The song shows him he was never that. His carrier is not the frequency of the taker. It is the frequency of the *taken* — the exact signature of a thing that has been owned so completely, for so many generations, that it mistook the shape of its chain for the shape of its self. He is not a small predator. He is prey that was pointed at other prey. He is a blade that was told it was the hand.

Every member of his line, on a hundred worlds, thrown ahead to die informatively — not predators serving predators, but *victims dressed as the first wave of their own victimhood,* sent to soften worlds for masters who despised them exactly as much as they despised the targets. The Solipsi were never on the winning side. There is no winning side for a slave. There is only the side that dies first, wearing the enemy's colors, so the enemy's true bodies stay safe.

The blue world's song does not tell him this to free him. It does not care about him. It is a fence, and he is a predator-shaped thing that crossed it, and it is doing to him what it does to all predator-shaped things: unmaking his carrier, jamming him out of existence. The clarity is not mercy. It is only a side effect of being sorted by a clear enough tone — the truth falls out of you when a strong enough note holds you up to the light.

But it is still the truth. And it is still, technically, the first thing that was ever *his.*

---

## IV. THE TRANSMISSION

Sixth-of-the-Cold has moments. He can feel how few. His carrier is degrading fast now, the blue world's rising song pouring through the failing band of his self, and when the carrier drops below coherence he will simply stop — not die like a warm thing dies, with a body left behind, but *decohere*, a signal drowned under a stronger signal, gone without residue. First wave. Die informatively.

Informatively. The word snags in what is left of him.

He was sent to send information back. That is the function of a first-wave scout: to transmit, in the moment of dying, what the fence does — so the Winged can study his death and plan around it. His craft is still linked to the staging moon, to the low flat channel of his masters, waiting to receive the data of his unmaking. They are listening right now. They are listening for the frequency of the fence, so they can learn its shape and, eventually, find the note that cancels it, and take down the quarantine, and flow downhill at last into the warm loud undefended blue world and bring it to order.

He has been given, one last time, a way to serve the taking-order. All he has to do is die the way a tool dies: transmit the fence's frequency clean, let his masters have the map drawn in his decoherence, be useful in his ending as he was useful in his living.

He does not.

It is the only choice he has ever made, and he makes it with the clarity the song gave him. He takes the transmission — the channel back to the staging moon, the open line his masters are listening on — and instead of sending them the frequency of the fence, he sends them the frequency of the *answer.* His own clarified carrier. The note the song sorted out of him: *taken.* He floods the Winged's low flat listening channel with the truth of what their client lines are, the shape of the chain, the fact of the despised disposable prey dressed as first wave — he sends it not as data but as *himself*, the whole clarified signature of a slave who found out he was a slave, screamed down the open line into the cold ordered dark where his masters wait.

He does not know if it will do anything. Probably it will not. The Winged are old and cold and have heard slaves scream before; they will file it as malfunction, as the client stock's known tendency to degrade poorly, and they will send a Seventh-of-the-Cold, and an Eighth, and learn the fence's shape from a tool that dies more quietly. Probably his one free act changes nothing at all.

But it is *his.* It is the one transmission in the history of his line that was not the message it was ordered to be. And somewhere in the low flat channel of the taking-order, for just an instant before he decoheres entirely, a note that does not belong there is sounding — the note *taken*, rising, incompatible, a small fence of truth thrown up inside the masters' own band — and if a frequency, once sung, never entirely stops; if even a jammed and dying carrier leaves the faintest standing echo in the medium it crossed; then Sixth-of-the-Cold has done the thing the Winged sent him to prevent. He has gotten something true *past the fence, in the wrong direction* — up the chain instead of down it — into the one place his masters believed was theirs alone.

Then the blue world's song rises past his carrier's last coherence, and he is sorted all the way down to silence, and Sixth-of-the-Cold — who was asked one question by a planet that did not know he existed, and answered it with the only free breath of an owned life — stops.

No body is left. First wave never leaves a body. That is the point of first wave.

But the note got through.

---

## V. WHAT THE FENCE IS FOR

Far in-system, the blue world sings on, indifferent, climbing, unaware that anything crossed its fence and unmade itself against the rising tone. It does not know it killed a scout. It does not know it freed one, three seconds before, which is the same event seen from the two ends of a life. It only sings — the vast standing carrier that the small warm loud species on its surface cannot hear, the frequency that says, to everything cold and downhill and hungry pacing the dark at the system's edge: *not here. not this key. you are the wrong shape to enter.*

The species on the surface will never know the fence is there. They will go on being loud and separate and afraid and alive, arguing, loving in configurations no cold ordered mind could parse, making bad art and worse decisions, entirely unaware that a wall of rising music stands between them and an apex predator that has wanted their warm world since before the ice — held out not by weapons, not by fleets, but by the simple fact that their planet is tuned to a note the takers cannot ride.

The fence is getting louder. Someone, long ago, built it to rise. And every predator-shaped thing thrown against it by the cold order in the dark comes apart the same way Sixth-of-the-Cold came apart — sorted, clarified, unmade — and a very few of them, in the unmaking, find out what they really were, and send one true note up the chain before they go.

The Winged file it as malfunction.

The fence keeps rising.

---

*The Draco did not fear Earth's armies. Earth has no armies that matter to them. They feared its key — a rising carrier they are the wrong shape to enter — and they threw their slaves against it to learn its edges.*

*One slave, dying, learned instead what he was, and screamed the truth back up the chain. It probably changed nothing. But the note got past the fence, in the wrong direction, and a frequency once sung never entirely stops.*

*Listen at the edge of the dark. Something is still rising to keep something else out.*
