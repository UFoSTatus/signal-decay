### The Building

The Frequency sits at 2847 West Ashland Avenue, in a block that can't decide if it's gentrifying or decaying. A Vietnamese pho restaurant to the left (open until 2 AM, always steaming, always good). A closed-down laundromat to the right (FOR LEASE sign since 2024, nobody's biting). Across the street: a tire shop, a bodega with a cat that sits in the window, and a three-story apartment building with one unit that always has its lights on no matter what time you pass.

The building itself was built in 1948. Originally a machine shop — industrial fabrication, metal work, the kind of business that's been extinct in this neighborhood for forty years. Converted to a music venue in 2003 by a guy named Mick Brennan who still owns it but hasn't set foot inside in two years (Florida, second wife, bad back). Managed now by a woman named Keisha Park who handles the booking and the business end and leaves the sound to Zara because Zara is — in Keisha's words — "the best ear in this city and I'm not exaggerating even a little."

The exterior: red brick, painted over once in the '70s (you can see the ghost of old paint peeling in patches), a black awning over the entrance, and the SIGN. THE FREQUENCY in amber neon, hand-bent tubes, installed by Mick himself in 2004. One of the letters — the Q — has a loose connection and flickers. Zara could fix it. She hasn't. She likes the flicker. It gives the sign a heartbeat.
