# WINGS NIGHT

*Life & Times Side-Quest · Kael · a weeknight, present day (2026)*
*Register: slice-of-life — a quiet friendship, the lightest undertone*
*Created: 2026-09-11*

*A standalone story. You need to have read nothing else. Two guys who work in a warehouse eat wings after a shift. One of them barely talks. It's one of the best friendships either of them has. Nothing happens.*

---

## CLOCK-OUT

Kael Voss clocked out at 6:10, same as every day, Dock 3, Bay 2, and Troy was waiting by the time clock the way Troy was always waiting, because Tuesday was wings night and had been wings night for going on two years, and Troy would sooner miss his own birthday than wings night.

Troy talked enough for both of them. This was the foundational fact of the friendship and the reason it worked. Troy was a big loud guy from a big loud family who could not physically tolerate a silence and had, over two years, arrived at the accidental genius of befriending the quietest man in the warehouse — because Kael never interrupted, never competed, never made Troy feel like he had to *earn* the floor. Troy talked; Kael listened; and Kael, it turned out, was the best listener Troy had ever had in his life, because Kael listened with his entire being, the way most people can't, the way that made you feel, when you had Kael's attention, like you were the only thing happening in the world.

Troy did not know why Kael was like this. Troy did not know that Kael listened that hard because listening was safer than talking, that Kael read the frequency under everyone's words, that Kael had spent his life in a silence that was a dam and not a desert. Troy just knew that when he told Kael something, Kael actually *heard* it, and that this was rare, and that he'd take a quiet friend who really listened over a loud one who was just waiting to talk any day of the week.

"Wings," Troy announced, as if it were news. "I'm thinkin' the mango habanero. I'm thinkin' I make a mistake with the mango habanero every single week and I do it again anyway because I'm a man of conviction. You still doin' the medium like a coward?"

Kael nodded.

"Coward," Troy said, with love, and they walked out to the lot.

---

## THE BAR

The place was called Winger's and it was exactly what it sounds like — sticky tables, too many TVs, a beer selection that was mostly one beer, a specific smell of fryer oil and Buffalo sauce that lived in your clothes for a day after. It was, objectively, not a nice place. It was, to Kael and Troy, close to sacred, in the way that a bad place you go to every week with the same person becomes sacred without anyone deciding it should.

They had a table. Not *a* table — *the* table, the one in the corner under the TV that showed whatever game, and if someone was sitting at it Troy took it as a personal affront and radiated wounded energy until they left. Tonight it was free. Troy exhaled like a man coming home.

Here is the one not-ordinary thing, and it is barely anything, and it's the whole reason Kael can stand a loud sports bar at all:

Kael heard everything. That was the truth of him in a place like this — the roar of a bar was, to someone who read frequency under sound, almost unbearable, a wall of overlapping voices and TV noise and fryer hiss and clinking glass, each carrying its little cargo of human feeling, all of it hitting him at once. Most crowded rooms were a kind of low pain. He'd learned to endure them, to build a wall, to hold the door.

But *this* room, with *Troy* in it, was different, and Kael had never figured out how to say why. Something about Troy's voice — big and warm and completely without hidden frequencies, a man who was exactly what he sounded like, no dam, no held door, nothing pressed down underneath — something about Troy's voice cut a clean channel through the noise. When Troy talked, the wall of the bar organized itself around him, and Kael could rest inside it. Troy's voice was, though neither of them would ever have words for it, a kind of shelter. The one loud thing in Kael's life that didn't hurt.

So Kael sat in the sacred corner booth and let Troy's voice make the roar bearable and ate his medium wings like a coward and was, in his silent way, completely happy.

---

## THE CONVERSATION (SUCH AS IT WAS)

Troy talked. He talked about the mango habanero, which was, as predicted, a mistake, and which he ate anyway, sweating and triumphant. He talked about his sister's wedding and the seating-chart politics thereof. He talked about a guy at the warehouse named Dennis who microwaved fish in the break room and how Dennis was, in Troy's considered opinion, a war criminal. He talked about the game on the TV, which neither of them cared about, narrating it purely for the pleasure of narration.

And Kael, across the sticky table, participated the way Kael participated: a nod, a raised eyebrow, the small rare smile that Troy had learned to treasure because it was so hard-won, a single word dropped in at exactly the right moment that landed harder than a paragraph because Kael spent his words like they were rationed, which they were.

At one point Troy wound down — even Troy wound down — and there was a lull, and Troy looked at his war-criminal wings and said, not really to Kael, just out loud, the way you say a true thing when you've had one beer and you're comfortable: "This is the best part of my week, man. I ever tell you that? Dumb wings at a dumb bar. Best part of my week."

And Kael — who did not do this, who almost never did this — looked at Troy across the table, and let the moment sit, and then said, quietly, four words, spending them:

"Mine too."

And he meant it more than Troy could ever know. Because Troy thought he was talking about wings. Kael was talking about the one loud room that didn't hurt, the one voice that cut a clean channel through the roar, the one person in a life of held-back silence who never once made Kael feel like he had to fill the quiet — who just talked, warm and easy, and let Kael rest inside it, and called him a coward with love, and never asked him to be anything other than the quiet guy who really listened.

Troy, not a man built to sit in a tender moment, immediately ruined it, which was also perfect: "Damn right it is. Now watch, I'm gonna order the atomic just to prove I don't learn."

He did. He didn't learn. It was a great night.

---

## THE DRIVE

After, Troy drove him home, because Kael's car was in the shop and Troy would've offered anyway. And here is the thing that told you everything about the friendship: Troy, who could not tolerate silence, drove Kael home in silence, and it was fine.

Not the whole way. Troy talked for a while, wound down, and then just... stopped, and turned the radio low, and drove, and let it be quiet. He'd learned, without ever discussing it, that Kael needed some quiet at the end of a loud day, and Troy — loud Troy, silence-hating Troy — gave it to him. Twenty minutes of a low radio and a dark highway and two guys who'd eaten too many wings, not talking, and it was one of the most comfortable silences Kael Voss had ever been inside.

Kael's whole life was silence. He was made of it. But most of his silence was the dam kind, the held-door kind, the kind that pressed. This — Troy's car, the low radio, the dark road, a friend who'd chosen to be quiet just for him — this was the other kind. The good kind. The kind that felt like company instead of a cage.

Troy dropped him at his door. "Same time next week," Troy said, which was not a question.

Kael nodded. Then, spending one more word because Troy had earned it: "Thanks."

"For the ride or the wings?"

Kael thought about it. "Both," he said, which for Kael was practically a speech, and Troy laughed and pulled away, and Kael stood in the dark for a second listening to the taillights fade into the ordinary night.

Then he went inside, into his own silence, the held kind, the 3 AM kind. But he carried something in with him from the loud warm bar and the quiet dark car — the specific full feeling of an evening with the one loud thing that didn't hurt. He slept a little better than usual. He didn't know why. He'd have said nothing happened.

Two guys ate wings. One of them barely talked. It was the best part of both their weeks.

*No powers, no Legacy, no furnace — just wings night. A quiet man and his loud best friend at a bad sports bar, then a comfortable silence on a dark drive home. The only trace of what Kael is: in a roaring room that should be unbearable to someone who hears the frequency under everything, his friend Troy's warm, uncomplicated voice cuts a clean channel he can rest inside — the one loud thing in his life that doesn't hurt. He'll spend four words to say "mine too," and mean the whole world by it. The band is made of these nights. Especially the quiet, wing-sauce-covered, unremarkable ones.*
