# CHARACTER PROFILE: THE GIRL WHO SEES

## WRITING RULE: BE EXTREMELY DESCRIPTIVE

Every scene in Sunny's story should be IMMERSIVE. Not plot summaries — lived experiences. The reader should FEEL like they're there.

**Always describe:**
- What the room/space LOOKS like (lighting, colors, objects, shadows)
- What it SMELLS like (abuela's cooking, old library books, the metallic cold when spirits appear, Hot Cheetos dust on her fingers)
- What it SOUNDS like (the hum of the refrigerator, Art Bell's voice crackling from the radio, the specific silence that means something is HERE)
- What it FEELS like (temperature shifts, the weight of her hoodie, the vinyl of the kitchen chair sticking to her thighs in summer, the cold prickle on her neck)
- What she's WEARING (specific — not "a hoodie" but "the oversized navy FUBU hoodie with the fraying cuff she chews when she's nervous")
- What she's EATING (specific — "the last three Gushers from the packet, the ones that burst warm and too-sweet because the box sat in the sun on the counter")
- What time of day / what the LIGHT is doing (the blue glow of the TV in a dark room, the orange streetlight through her blinds, the gray nothing of a winter morning)
- How spirits MANIFEST sensorily (not just "she saw a ghost" — describe the temperature drop, the pressure change in her ears, the way the air tastes like old pennies, how the light bends slightly at the edges of where they stand)

**Examples of descriptive vs. flat:**

❌ FLAT: "She saw a ghost in the hallway at school."

✅ DESCRIPTIVE: "The hallway smelled like floor wax and wet sneakers and then — underneath — something older. Cold. Like opening a window in January. Sunny's fingers tightened on her Trapper Keeper. The fluorescent lights above Ms. Patterson's door flickered once, twice, and there she was: the woman in the blue dress, walking that same diagonal path from the water fountain to the wall. Not through the wall. INTO it. Like the wall wasn't there. Or like she remembered a door that used to be. Her shoes made no sound. Her dress didn't move with her steps. And she never, not once in two years of walking that path, looked at Sunny. Which was how Sunny knew she wasn't real. The real ones always look."

❌ FLAT: "She felt scared at the sleepover."

✅ DESCRIPTIVE: "Kayla's bedroom smelled like Bath & Body Works cucumber melon and the pizza rolls they'd microwaved an hour ago. The sleeping bags made a nylon sound every time someone shifted. Sunny was in the purple one, the one Kayla's mom pulled from the hall closet that smelled faintly like mothballs. The other girls were asleep — she could tell by their breathing, slow and heavy, mouths open. The TV was still on, blue light pulsing from the Blockbuster menu screen on repeat. And in the corner by Kayla's desk, where the Beanie Babies were lined up on the shelf, a man was standing. Old. Overalls. Hands at his sides. Looking at Kayla. Not angry. Not sad. Just... looking. Like he'd been looking for a long time. Sunny pulled the sleeping bag up to her nose and breathed mothball air and tried to decide if this was a 'tell Kayla's mom' situation or a 'pretend to be asleep until morning' situation."

**This level of description applies to EVERYTHING:**
- Normal life scenes (not just ghost scenes)
- Food scenes (describe the TASTE, the texture, how it feels to eat when you're scared vs. when you're happy)
- Emotional moments (describe what emotion feels like PHYSICALLY — tight chest, hot eyes, that weird hollow feeling behind the sternum)
- 90s details (specific brands, specific products, specific sounds of that era — the dial-up screech, the click of a VHS going in, the weight of a corded phone)

## ERA: THE 1990s

**Born: 1985. Story spans 1993-2000 (ages 8-15).**

This is a 90s story. That matters. Because the 90s meant:

### What She DOESN'T Have:
- No Google. Can't search "why do I see dead people."
- No Reddit. No forums (until maybe 1999, and she'd need to know they exist).
- No YouTube. No TikTok. No community of people posting about their experiences.

### What She DOES Have:

- **GHOST SHOWS.** This is her era. The 90s was the GOLDEN AGE of paranormal TV. She watches ALL of it:

  **The Big Ones (her education):**
  - **Unsolved Mysteries** (1987-2002) — Robert Stack's trenchcoat and voice. The ghost segments VALIDATED her. She's 7 when she starts watching. Those reenactments are cheesy but the stories are REAL. She takes notes.
  - **Sightings** (1992-1997) — Paranormal newsmagazine. On when she's 7-12. Treated the paranormal like NEWS, not entertainment. This is where she first heard terminology: "residual haunting," "intelligent spirit," "poltergeist activity." It gave her LANGUAGE for what she experiences.
  - **The X-Files** (1993-2002) — She's 8 when it premieres. Mulder BELIEVES. In a world of Scullys telling her she's imagining things, Mulder says "the truth is out there." She has an "I Want to Believe" poster by age 10. It's not about aliens for her — it's about someone on TV taking the unexplained SERIOUSLY.
  - **Beyond Belief: Fact or Fiction** (1997-2002) — Jonathan Frakes presenting stories and asking "which are real?" She always knows. ALWAYS. She watches with her abuela and calls every single one correctly. Abuela just smiles.
  - **In Search Of...** (reruns airing constantly in the 90s) — Leonard Nimoy narrating paranormal investigations. Calming. Academic. Makes her feel like what she does could be STUDIED, not feared.

  **The Kids' Shows (validation through fiction):**
  - **Are You Afraid of the Dark?** (1991-1996) — Nickelodeon. The Midnight Society telling ghost stories. She loves it AND hates it. Some episodes are so close to her real life she has to turn it off. But it normalizes talking about ghosts for kids her age.
  - **Ghostwriter** (1992-1995) — PBS. A ghost that communicates through text. She WISHES her ghosts were that chill.
  - **Goosebumps** (1995-1998) — R.L. Stine adaptations. She reads every book. The ghost ones make her laugh because they get so much WRONG. But she appreciates that kids her age are consuming ghost content without being traumatized.
  - **So Weird** (1999-2001) — Disney Channel. A girl who investigates paranormal stuff. By the time this airs she's 14 and thinks "finally. A show about a girl like me."

  **The Late Night Stuff (her secret life):**
  - **Coast to Coast AM with Art Bell** — Late-night radio (10 PM - 2 AM). She listens under her covers with a little clock radio. People calling in about EXACTLY what she experiences. Shadow people. Spirits. Hauntings. Psychic kids. Art Bell takes every caller seriously. This is the closest thing to community she has for YEARS. This show kept her SANE from ages 9-13.
  - **Strange Universe** (1996-1998) — Syndicated paranormal show. Airs late in her market.
  - **The Other Side** (1994-1995) — Short-lived NBC show about the paranormal.
  - Random 2 AM infomercial breaks where psychic hotline ads play — Miss Cleo, Dionne Warwick's Psychic Friends Network. She watches these with a mix of "that's fake" and "but what if ONE of them is real like me?"

  **What the shows gave her:**
  - VOCABULARY. Before Sightings, she had no words for what she saw. After: "residual," "intelligent," "poltergeist," "cold spot," "EVP," "ectoplasm."
  - VALIDATION. Every episode was proof that other people experienced this. Adults. Investigators. SCIENTISTS sometimes.
  - FRAMEWORK. The shows (especially Sightings and Unsolved Mysteries) presented investigations with METHOD. She learned to approach her own experiences like an investigator, not a victim.
  - HOPE. If Robert Stack was taking this seriously on primetime NBC, maybe she wasn't crazy after all.
  - LIMITATIONS. She also learned what the shows get WRONG. The reenactments are dramatic but inaccurate. The "psychics" on some shows are clearly fake. She can tell. She develops a filter: entertainment vs. truth.

  **The show that BROKE her open (1996-1997):**
  Not a ghost show — a TALK SHOW. Montel Williams or Maury or Sally Jesse Raphael does an episode on "Children Who See Ghosts." Real kids. Real tears. A psychic medium on the panel looks at a crying 11-year-old and says: "Sweetheart, this isn't a punishment. You have a gift." Sunny is 11, home from school sick, watching daytime TV. She hits RECORD on the VCR. Watches that segment 30+ times over the next year. That VHS tape = sacred object.

- **The Sixth Sense (1999).** When this movie comes out, she's 14. She sits in the theater and CRIES. "I see dead people." Someone put it in a MOVIE. It's real enough for Hollywood. She's not alone.

## THE TURNING POINT: "I'M NOT THE ONLY ONE"

For THREE YEARS (age 8-11) she thought she was being punished.

She thought God was angry at her. She thought she did something wrong as a baby and this was her consequence. She thought maybe she was cursed. Maybe her family was cursed. Maybe she was going crazy like her tía Lucia who nobody talks about. Every night she prayed for it to stop. It never stopped.

**The real torture wasn't the ghosts. It was the ISOLATION.**

She couldn't tell anyone. Mom didn't fully believe her. Friends would think she was insane. Teachers would call the school counselor. She was ALONE with this thing that no child should carry alone. And she was 9. 10. 11. Every single day, alone with the dead, thinking she was the only person in the world this happened to.

Then — age 11, channel surfing at 2 AM because she couldn't sleep (a spirit in the hallway was being loud) — she lands on a show. Kids. On TV. Talking about seeing spirits. KIDS HER AGE. Crying about it. Scared of it. And an adult — a man with kind eyes — looking at them and saying:

> *"This isn't a punishment. This is a gift. You're not broken. You're not crazy. You see more than other people. And you're not alone."*

She sat on her abuela's couch at 2:17 AM and SOBBED.

Not because she was sad. Because for the first time in three years, she wasn't alone. There were other kids. LOTS of them. Kids who saw the same things. Kids who were scared like her. Kids whose parents didn't believe them. Kids who thought they were being punished too.

And an adult — finally, ONE adult — saying: this is real. You're not imagining it. And it's not a curse.

**What changed:**
- She stopped praying for it to go away. Started asking "okay, what do I DO with this?"
- She went online. Found forums. Found communities. Found adults who'd had it since childhood.
- She stopped thinking of herself as broken. Started thinking of herself as DIFFERENT. Then eventually: gifted.
- The show didn't teach her HOW to control it (she still had to figure that out alone). But it taught her the most important thing: **she wasn't the only one.** And that changed EVERYTHING.
- Abuela saw her watching the show and quietly sat down next to her. Didn't say anything. Just put her hand on Sunny's knee. That's when Sunny KNEW abuela understood. They never had a big dramatic conversation about it. They didn't need to.

**The shift in her notebook:**
- Before the show: entries are scared, angry, pleading. "WHY ME?" "Please stop." "I hate this."
- After the show: entries become CURIOUS. "What kind was that one?" "Why do they come at night more?" "Theory: temperature affects visibility."
- She went from victim to RESEARCHER in one night. All because a man on TV said "gift, not curse."

## THE SIGNAL DECAY CONNECTION: SHE FEELS FREQUENCIES

Here's what makes Sunny RARE — even among Sensitives who see spirits:

**Most ghost-seers just SEE.** Visual. A spirit appears and they perceive it visually — form, shape, transparency. That's it. Standard Thanatological Sensitivity.

**Sunny FEELS the frequency.**

Every spirit, every entity, every type of manifestation vibrates at a distinct frequency — and she can SENSE that frequency directly. Not just see the ghost. FEEL its wavelength. Like how a musician hears pitch, she feels the frequency signature of dead things.

**What this means in practice:**

| Entity Type | What It FEELS Like to Her | Frequency Range |
|------------|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Residual (Loops) | A low, steady hum. Monotone. Repetitive. Like a washing machine in another room. | Fixed, unchanging — 12-18 Hz |
| Intelligent (kind) | A warm pulse. Irregular, alive-feeling. Like a heartbeat that shouldn't be there. | Variable, 20-45 Hz |
| Intelligent (confused) | Static. Fuzzy. Like a radio between stations. Uncomfortable to feel. | Unstable, jumping 15-50 Hz |
| Angry Ones | A sharp, jagged vibration. Like touching an electric fence. Hurts. | Spiky, 55-80 Hz |
| Poltergeist (living source) | Heat. Pressure. Like standing next to a subwoofer turned too loud. Comes from the PERSON. | Chaotic, 30-100 Hz surges |
| The Dark Ones | ABSENCE. Not a frequency — a HOLE where frequency should be. The silence that isn't silence. Nothing. | Below 0 Hz / anti-frequency |

**She can tell what something IS before she even SEES it** — because she feels the frequency first. She walks into a haunted building and knows WHAT'S in there by the feel before anything appears. That's her edge. That's what makes her more than just a ghost-seer.

**She can also feel when frequencies CHANGE:**
- When a Stuck One is ready to pass on, their frequency smooths out — becomes harmonious. She feels them "tuning up" before they leave.
- When an Angry One is about to escalate, the jagged spikes get sharper. She feels the danger coming.
- When a poltergeist source (a living person) is about to blow, the pressure builds until it's unbearable.
- **When The Five play for the first time:** EVERY frequency she's ever felt shifts simultaneously. Like someone changed the key signature of reality. Every spirit turns the same direction. Every Loop stutters. Every Angry One goes quiet. For 3.7 seconds: perfect, terrifying harmony. Then everything is LOUDER. Stronger. Closer. Something fundamental changed in the frequency architecture of the world. She felt it in her BONES. She's 13 and she doesn't know what happened. But she knows it was BIG.

**Why Legacy watches her:** She's classified THETA-7 (Thanatological Sensitive) — low priority. But her frequency-perception adds a secondary classification they've never seen before: THETA-7/SIGMA (Frequency-Aware Thanatological Sensitive). There are only 3 known in the world. If the Dampener ever fails, she won't just see more ghosts — she'll perceive the ENTIRE frequency architecture of the death-transition layer. The infrastructure of what happens to consciousness when it leaves the body. That knowledge would make her either incredibly powerful or completely insane.

## HOW HER POWER WORKS (The Matilda Connection)

Like Matilda's telekinesis, Sunny's ability is tied to her MIND:

- **The more she understands, the more she can do.** When she didn't understand what she was seeing (age 8), she had no control — spirits just appeared and she could only watch. As she studied, categorized, and COMPREHENDED the phenomenon, her ability grew.
- **Knowledge = Power (literally).** Each category she figured out gave her more ability to interact with that type. Understanding Loops meant she could tune them out. Understanding Intelligent spirits meant she could communicate deliberately. Understanding poltergeists meant she could see the energy source.
- **Her brain is too big for her world.** Like Matilda whose genius needed an outlet (telekinesis), Sunny's mind processes so much information about the death-transition layer that it developed the ability to INTERACT with it, not just observe.
- **She could theoretically get MUCH stronger.** If she understood the full frequency architecture (which she can't yet — the Dampener limits this, and she's 13), her ability could evolve from "seeing spirits" to "navigating the entire Thanatological Layer at will." That's terrifying. That's also Season 3+ material.

## HOW HER POWER WORKS (The Velma Connection)

Like Velma solving mysteries, Sunny INVESTIGATES:

- **She approaches each encounter like a case.** Who is this spirit? When did they die? What do they want? What type are they? She gathers evidence before acting.
- **She's wrong sometimes.** And she learns from being wrong. Velma's "I would have gotten away with it" moments — Sunny has her own. Times she misclassified a spirit and responded incorrectly. Those mistakes taught her more than her successes.
- **She explains things to herself out loud.** Talking through her observations. Narrating her logic. "Okay. Doesn't respond to me. Same path every time. Time-locked to 3:47 PM. This is a Loop. Don't engage."
- **She wants PROOF.** For herself. That she's not imagining this. She photographs her temperature readings. She writes everything down. She cross-references. She's building a BODY OF EVIDENCE that her experience is real — even if nobody else will ever see it.
- **"Jinkies" energy.** When she figures out what a spirit wants, or why they're stuck, or how to help them — there's a flash of genuine intellectual EXCITEMENT before the emotional weight lands. She loves solving the puzzle. Even when the puzzle is heartbreaking.
