## 1.4 — The 10,000-Hour Threshold

Permanence occurs at approximately 30 mR accumulated residue. At the standard emotional-playing absorption rate of 0.003 mR/hr, this equals:

```
30 mR ÷ 0.003 mR/hr = 10,000 hours of emotional playing
```

For a professional musician who plays with genuine emotion for 3 hours per day (not total playing time — EMOTIONAL playing time):

```
10,000 hours ÷ 3 hours/day = 3,333 days ≈ 9.1 years
```

**But this is an AVERAGE.** Variables that accelerate imbuing:

- **Intensity of emotion:** Grief deposits ~2x standard rate. Rage ~1.8x. Ecstatic joy ~1.5x. Quiet melancholy ~1.2x. Sexual/romantic energy ~1.4x.
- **Audience presence:** Playing to an audience that is emotionally engaged creates a FEEDBACK LOOP. The audience's collective consciousness-frequency bounces back to the performer, amplifying their output. Live performance imbues at 1.5-3x the rate of solo playing.
- **Acoustics of the space:** Small, reverberant rooms hold frequency longer, creating standing waves that repeatedly wash over the instrument. A musician playing in a small club every night imbues faster than one playing outdoor festivals.
- **Physical contact intensity:** Pressing harder, playing louder, more body contact with the instrument = more direct frequency transfer. Finger-style guitarists imbue faster than pick players. Pianists who play fortissimo imbue keys faster.
- **Proximity to death:** Musicians playing in the last months/weeks of their life deposit at DRAMATICALLY accelerated rates (up to 10x). The consciousness-frequency output of a dying person is immense — their soul is already loosening from the body. (This is why instruments played by musicians who died young are so powerfully imbued despite fewer total hours.)

**Below-permanence residue DOES decay** — at approximately 0.0001 mR per day. An instrument that isn't played emotionally will slowly lose accumulated residue. This is why instruments left in cases for decades feel "dead" when picked up again — the residue has faded below perceptibility. But if the instrument reached permanence (30+ mR), the residue is locked. Forever. Even if the instrument sits in a vault for a thousand years.

These preferences include:

| Preference Type | Manifestation |
|----------------|---------------|
| **Favorite keys** | The instrument resonates more richly in keys the original player favored. A guitar imbued by a blues player in E and A will literally produce fuller overtones in those keys. |
| **Comfortable tempos** | The residue creates micro-resistance at tempos the original player didn't use. Playing at the "wrong" tempo feels like pushing through syrup. |

| **Tonal preferences** | The residue "likes" certain amp settings, string types, pick thicknesses. Using the "wrong" equipment creates a subtle friction. |

## 2.6 — Layered Residue

An instrument that has been played emotionally by MULTIPLE musicians over decades develops LAYERED residue — stratified deposits of different consciousness-frequencies, one atop another.

Layered residue is more complex than single-player residue:
- The layers can be COMPLEMENTARY (multiple blues players on the same guitar = reinforced blues tendency)
- The layers can be CONTRADICTORY (a jazz player's residue atop a metal player's = confusing, chaotic suggestions)
- The layers can be CHRONOLOGICAL (the most recent layer is "loudest" — closest to the surface — but older layers still exist beneath)
- Rarely, layers can FUSE — two compatible residues merging into a single, more complex pattern

Instruments with many complementary layers are the most powerful. A violin played by five generations of virtuosos, each building on the last, each reinforcing the same tradition — that instrument is a cathedral of accumulated mastery.

Instruments with contradictory layers are unstable. They send mixed signals to the player. This can be creatively INTERESTING — forcing the player to navigate conflicting suggestions, producing unexpected hybrid styles — or it can be maddening, producing a sense that the instrument "can't make up its mind."

## 7.4 — Instrument Destruction: What Happens to Released Frequency?

**The question:** When an imbued instrument is destroyed — burned, smashed, crushed, dissolved — what happens to the accumulated residue?

**Observed outcomes (Legacy documentation):**

### Scenario A: Gradual Destruction (rot, decay, neglect)
The residue dissipates SLOWLY as the physical structure degrades. Frequency bleeds out over years/decades as the wood decomposes, the metal corrodes, the structure loses integrity. By the time the instrument is truly "gone," the residue has already dispersed into the surrounding environment at levels too low to affect anything.

**Result:** Harmless dissipation. The frequency is lost. The ghost fades.

### Scenario B: Sudden Violent Destruction (smashing, burning, explosive)
The residue is RELEASED in a burst. Not gradually — all at once. A lifetime (or centuries) of accumulated consciousness-frequency, compressed into the material, suddenly has no material to inhabit.

**Observed effects:**
- Electromagnetic pulse at the moment of destruction (instruments with 50+ mR have caused power fluctuations in nearby buildings)
- Auditory phenomenon: witnesses report hearing "a sound like screaming" or "like a chord being played backwards" — even if the instrument wasn't plugged in, even if it had no strings
- Psychological effects on witnesses: temporary emotional flooding (feeling the CONTENTS of the residue all at once — grief, joy, rage, love, whatever was stored)
- In extreme cases (100+ mR): the released frequency can IMPLANT TEMPORARILY in nearby humans. Witnesses to the destruction of heavily imbued instruments report days-long episodes of "inherited memories" — fragments of the dead musician's emotional life, playing on repeat in their consciousness

### Scenario C: Destruction of Anomalous Instruments (Category 5-6)
Unknown. The Legacy has never intentionally destroyed a Category 5+ instrument. The risk assessment suggests that releasing that much concentrated (and possibly active) frequency could:
- Permanently alter the consciousness of anyone within radius
- Create a lasting "frequency scar" in the location (a zone of permanent emotional/perceptual distortion)
- If a Hendrix guitar were destroyed: possibly trigger a HARMONIC EVENT — a burst of previous-cycle frequency that could activate dormant abilities in nearby humans

**This is why the Legacy CONTAINS rather than DESTROYS.** Destruction isn't safe for high-category instruments. The frequency has to go SOMEWHERE. And "somewhere" might be worse than "contained in a guitar in a vault."

### Scenario D: The Fragments
When an instrument is broken into pieces but not DESTROYED — each piece retains a PORTION of the residue proportional to its mass and density. A guitar smashed into pieces scatters its residue across the fragments. Each fragment broadcasts at reduced power. Each fragment could potentially imbue a new instrument if incorporated into one (a fingerboard salvaged from a smashed guitar, installed on a new neck, carries its fraction of residue into the new instrument).

This creates a disturbing possibility: deliberate fragmenting as a method of creating MULTIPLE weakly-imbued artifacts from one strongly-imbued source. The Legacy has strict protocols against this. Private collectors and corporations may not.

# APPENDIX D: RULES FOR WRITERS

When writing scenes involving imbued instruments in Signal Decay, these rules apply:

1. **NEVER make the imbuing OBVIOUS to characters** (except Zara, and even she doesn't know the full picture). Characters feel the effects but attribute them to other causes. The reader knows. The characters don't.

2. **The instruments don't talk.** They don't glow. They don't move. They're INSTRUMENTS. The influence is internal — felt in the fingers, in the emotions, in the creative impulses. It's never visible to an observer.

3. **The instruments can't be "wrong."** If a character plays their imbued instrument and sounds bad — that's the CHARACTER's limitation, not the instrument's. Imbuing amplifies ability; it doesn't REPLACE it.

4. **Resistance is always possible.** A character can play against their instrument's preferences. It just feels harder. Like swimming upstream. Occasionally this is dramatically appropriate — forcing a bright guitar to play something dark, fighting the residue's suggestion, creating friction that becomes its own kind of energy.

5. **The instruments are morally neutral.** Even "dark-imbued" instruments aren't evil. They're carrying the frequency of human pain. They're artifacts of suffering, not agents of malice. Don't write them as cursed objects. Write them as scar tissue — healed over, but still sensitive.

6. **The PLAYER still matters most.** An imbued instrument in the hands of someone with no ability produces nothing. The human is always the source. The instrument is always the amplifier. Never forget which is which.

7. **Music details should be SPECIFIC.** When describing the effect of imbuing, use real musical terminology: specific keys, specific intervals, specific techniques, specific tempos, specific tone descriptors. Vagueness kills the illusion. This universe is built by someone who KNOWS gear. Honor that.

8. **The body remembers.** When a character has been playing an imbued instrument for a long time and then plays something else (or nothing), their BODY retains the physical habits the residue encouraged. Muscle memory shaped by residue-suggestion. Even without the instrument, those patterns persist in the player. This is a subtle but important point: the instrument shapes the player over time, and that shaping doesn't vanish when the instrument is put down.
